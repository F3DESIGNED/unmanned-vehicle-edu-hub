# Ethics & Sustainability in Unmanned Systems

As we teach students to build and operate unmanned vehicles, we must also teach them to consider the ethical implications and environmental impact of this powerful technology.

## Why This Matters

Drone technology, like all powerful tools, can be used for tremendous good or potential harm. Students must understand:

- **Ethical responsibilities** of technology creators and operators
- **Privacy implications** of aerial surveillance capabilities
- **Environmental impact** of manufacturing and operations
- **Societal effects** of automation and job displacement
- **Regulatory frameworks** designed to protect public interests

## Integration Throughout Curriculum

Ethics and sustainability should not be isolated topics but integrated throughout:

### Beginner Level
- Safety as ethical responsibility
- Respecting privacy during flights
- Following regulations (introduction to why they exist)
- Basic environmental awareness

### Intermediate Level
- Privacy considerations in data collection
- Energy efficiency in mission planning
- Noise impact awareness
- Proper waste disposal (batteries, etc.)

### Advanced Level
- Ethical implications of AI and autonomy
- Dual-use technology discussions
- Environmental lifecycle analysis
- Societal impact of automation

## Learning Objectives

Students completing this framework will be able to:

1. **Analyze ethical implications** of drone applications
2. **Evaluate privacy concerns** and implement protections
3. **Assess environmental impact** of drone operations
4. **Design sustainably** with lifecycle considerations
5. **Engage in informed discussions** about technology policy
6. **Apply ethical frameworks** to real-world scenarios
7. **Minimize environmental footprint** of projects
8. **Advocate responsibly** for beneficial applications

## Discussion-Based Learning

Many topics in this framework are best learned through:

- **Case study analysis**: Real-world ethical dilemmas
- **Structured debates**: Multiple perspectives on complex issues
- **Socratic seminars**: Student-led discussions
- **Scenario analysis**: "What would you do?" situations
- **Guest speakers**: Ethicists, regulators, industry professionals

## Assessment Approaches

Ethical reasoning is assessed differently than technical skills:

- **Discussion participation**: Quality of contributions and reasoning
- **Written reflections**: Thoughtful analysis of ethical issues
- **Position papers**: Well-reasoned arguments with evidence
- **Scenario responses**: Applying ethical frameworks
- **Project integration**: Ethical considerations in technical work

## Building Ethical Technologists

Our goal is to graduate students who:

- **Think critically** about technology's role in society
- **Consider consequences** beyond immediate functionality
- **Value diverse perspectives** on technology impact
- **Take responsibility** for their creations
- **Advocate for beneficial use** of technology
- **Minimize harm** to people and environment
- **Engage constructively** in policy discussions
