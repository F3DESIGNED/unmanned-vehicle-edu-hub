# School Policies for Unmanned Vehicle Programs

!!! info "For Educators and Administrators"
    This guide helps schools, universities, and educational institutions establish safe, compliant unmanned vehicle programs.

## Why Formal Policies Matter

Educational institutions introducing unmanned vehicle systems need clear policies for:

- **Student safety** - Protecting students from injury
- **Legal compliance** - Meeting FAA and local regulations
- **Liability management** - Reducing institutional risk
- **Insurance requirements** - Satisfying carrier policies
- **Parent/guardian communication** - Transparency and consent
- **Consistent operations** - Fair, predictable procedures
- **Educational excellence** - Supporting learning objectives

**Informal programs without policies create legal and safety risks.**

## Policy Development Process

### Step 1: Stakeholder Engagement

Involve all relevant parties:

- **Administration** - Principal, superintendent, board
- **Faculty** - Teachers who will use platforms
- **Safety personnel** - School resource officer, safety coordinator
- **Legal counsel** - Review liability and compliance
- **Insurance carrier** - Confirm coverage
- **IT department** - Network, privacy, data concerns
- **Facilities** - Operating and storage locations
- **Parents/guardians** - Community input and consent

### Step 2: Risk Assessment

Evaluate your specific context:

**Institution type:**
- K-12 school (more restrictions)
- University/college (more flexibility)
- After-school program
- Summer camp

**Intended use:**
- Classroom demonstrations
- Student projects
- Research activities
- Competitions
- Promotional/marketing

**Platforms:**
- Indoor micro drones
- Outdoor UAVs
- Ground rovers
- Marine vehicles

**Student population:**
- Age ranges
- Maturity levels
- Special needs considerations

### Step 3: Draft Policy

Use templates below as starting point, customize for your institution.

### Step 4: Review and Approval

- Legal review
- Insurance review
- Administrative approval
- Board approval (if required)

### Step 5: Implementation

- Staff training
- Student orientation
- Parent communication
- Equipment procurement
- Documentation systems

### Step 6: Annual Review

- Review incidents
- Update for regulatory changes
- Assess effectiveness
- Revise as needed

## Sample Policy Template: K-12 School

### [School Name] Unmanned Aircraft Systems Policy

**Effective Date:** [Date]
**Review Date:** [Annual review date]
**Policy Owner:** [Title/Department]

---

#### 1. Purpose

This policy establishes guidelines for the safe, legal, and educational use of Unmanned Aircraft Systems (UAS), commonly called drones, at [School Name].

#### 2. Scope

This policy applies to:
- All students, staff, and volunteers
- All UAS operations on school property
- All school-sponsored UAS activities (on or off campus)
- All UAS used for school purposes

#### 3. Definitions

- **UAS/Drone:** Unmanned aircraft and associated equipment
- **Operator:** Person controlling the UAS
- **Visual Observer:** Person assisting operator by maintaining visual contact
- **School property:** All buildings, grounds, and facilities owned or controlled by the school

#### 4. Authorized Use

UAS may be used at [School Name] for:
- ✅ Educational instruction in STEM subjects
- ✅ Student projects under faculty supervision
- ✅ School-approved clubs and activities
- ✅ Facilities inspection and documentation
- ✅ Promotional photography/videography

UAS may **NOT** be used for:
- ❌ Personal recreation during school hours
- ❌ Unsupervised student operation
- ❌ Operations over school buildings during occupancy
- ❌ Any activity violating student privacy

#### 5. Regulatory Compliance

All UAS operations must comply with:
- Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) regulations
- State and local laws
- School district policies
- This institutional policy

**Required certifications:**
- Faculty operators: FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate
- Student operators (recreational): FAA TRUST test completion
- All platforms >250g: FAA registration

#### 6. Authorized Personnel

**Faculty/Staff Operators:**
- Must possess FAA Part 107 certificate
- Must complete school-specific UAS training (annually)
- Must be designated as "Authorized UAS Operator" by administration

**Student Operators:**
- Must complete FAA TRUST test (recreational operations)
- Must complete [Student Safety Training](student-safety-training.md) program
- Must operate under direct supervision of Authorized UAS Operator
- Parent/guardian consent required (see Section 9)

**Age restrictions:**
- Minimum age 12 for supervised operation
- Students under 12: observation only
- Part 107 certificate (age 16+) required for independent research use

#### 7. Approved Platforms

**Tier 1: Classroom Use (Unrestricted)**
- Weight: Under 250 grams
- Example: DJI Tello, Ryze Tello
- Use: Indoor instruction, programming lessons
- Supervision: Faculty present

**Tier 2: Outdoor Educational Use (Restricted)**
- Weight: 250g - 2kg
- Examples: DJI Mini series, custom educational builds
- Use: Outdoor supervised projects
- Supervision: Authorized Operator, designated flight area
- Requires: Flight plan approval

**Tier 3: Advanced/Research (Special Approval)**
- Weight: Over 2kg or specialized equipment
- Use: Advanced research, inspections
- Approval: Administrator and Legal Counsel review required

**Prohibited:**
- First-person view (FPV) racing drones
- High-speed racing platforms
- Any platform modified beyond manufacturer specifications (without approval)

#### 8. Operating Procedures

**Pre-Flight Requirements:**
- Completed pre-flight checklist (see [Safety Procedures](safety-procedures.md))
- Weather check (no operations in rain, wind >15mph, or low visibility)
- Airspace verification (B4UFLY app or equivalent)
- Flight plan filed with [designated administrator]

**Operating Restrictions:**
- **Hours:** Daylight only (30 min after sunrise to 30 min before sunset)
- **Altitude:** Maximum 100 feet AGL on campus (well below FAA 400' limit)
- **Distance:** Within visual line of sight at all times
- **Location:** Pre-approved flight areas only
- **Proximity:**
  - No operations over occupied buildings
  - 50 feet minimum from people not involved in operation
  - 100 feet minimum from parking lots, playgrounds, athletic fields (when in use)

**Required Equipment:**
- Fire extinguisher (ABC type) at operating location
- First aid kit
- LiPo safe bag for all battery charging
- High-visibility vest for operator (outdoor operations)

#### 9. Student Participation

**Parent/Guardian Consent:**
All students participating in UAS activities must have signed parent/guardian consent (annually). Consent form must include:
- Description of activities
- Safety procedures
- Risks and risk mitigation
- Privacy policies
- Insurance information
- Emergency contact update

**Student Training:**
Students must complete [Student Safety Training](student-safety-training.md) program covering:
- UAS basics and components
- FAA regulations (TRUST test)
- Battery safety (LiPo handling)
- Operating procedures
- Emergency response

**Supervision Ratios:**
- Indoor micro drones: 1 adult per 10 students
- Outdoor operations: 1 certified adult per 5 students actively operating

#### 10. Safety Requirements

**Mandatory Compliance:**
- All [Safety Procedures](safety-procedures.md) followed
- [Battery Safety](battery-safety.md) protocols enforced
- [FAA Regulations](faa-regulations.md) compliance verified

**Designated Charging Area:**
- Fire-resistant location (tile floor, away from combustibles)
- Smoke detector present
- Fire extinguisher within 10 feet
- Adult supervision required for all charging
- LiPo safe bags or metal containers used

**Incident Reporting:**
All incidents (crashes, injuries, near-misses, flyaways) must be reported to [designated administrator] within 24 hours. Serious incidents require immediate notification.

#### 11. Privacy and Data

**Image/Video Collection:**
- No operation with cameras over areas where students have reasonable expectation of privacy (locker rooms, bathrooms, etc.)
- Footage of students requires consent (covered in participation form)
- Data storage complies with district policies

**Data Security:**
- Flight logs and imagery stored per district data retention policies
- Access limited to authorized personnel
- Sensitive data encrypted

#### 12. Insurance and Liability

- School maintains liability insurance covering UAS operations
- All operators must be listed with insurance carrier
- Students covered under school's general liability policy during approved activities
- Certificate of insurance available upon request

**Student/Parent Acknowledgment:**
Participation forms include liability waiver (reviewed by legal counsel).

#### 13. Maintenance and Inspections

**Equipment Maintenance:**
- Monthly inspections by Authorized Operators
- Maintenance log maintained
- Damaged equipment removed from service immediately

**Program Review:**
- Annual policy review by [committee]
- Incident log review (quarterly)
- Equipment inventory audit (semester)

#### 14. Enforcement

**Policy Violations:**

Minor violations (e.g., incomplete checklist):
- Verbal warning
- Retraining required

Moderate violations (e.g., operation outside designated area):
- Written warning
- Temporary suspension of privileges
- Parental notification (students)

Serious violations (e.g., reckless operation, privacy invasion):
- Immediate suspension of privileges
- Disciplinary action per student handbook / employee policies
- Possible legal action

#### 15. Contact Information

**UAS Program Coordinator:** [Name, email, phone]
**Safety Officer:** [Name, email, phone]
**Emergency:** 911, then notify school office [phone]

---

**Approval Signatures:**

Principal: _________________________ Date: _________

Superintendent: _________________________ Date: _________

(Board approval if required)

## Sample Policy Template: University/College

### [University Name] Unmanned Vehicle Systems Policy

*(Abbreviated - adapt K-12 template above with these differences)*

**Key Differences from K-12:**

1. **Expanded Purpose:**
   - Research activities
   - Academic competitions
   - Student organizations (robotics clubs, etc.)
   - Commercial applications (with additional approval)

2. **Operator Categories:**
   - **Faculty/Staff:** Part 107 required for campus operations
   - **Graduate students:** May obtain Part 107 for research
   - **Undergraduate students:** Supervised operations, TRUST required
   - **Student organizations:** Designated Part 107 holder required

3. **Approved Platforms:**
   - More flexibility for research-grade equipment
   - Custom-built platforms allowed with engineering review
   - Ground and marine vehicles included

4. **Research Provisions:**
   - IRB approval required if human subjects involved
   - Data use protocols
   - Publication rights
   - Intellectual property considerations

5. **Competition Support:**
   - Travel policies for off-campus competitions
   - University insurance coverage verification
   - Team registration procedures

6. **Operating Areas:**
   - Designated outdoor flight areas
   - Indoor flight cage/facility (if available)
   - Off-campus operations (require additional approval)

## Implementation Checklist

For schools starting a UAS program:

### Phase 1: Planning (2-3 months before launch)
- [ ] Form working group (stakeholders)
- [ ] Conduct risk assessment
- [ ] Research insurance requirements
- [ ] Draft policy using templates
- [ ] Budget for equipment and training

### Phase 2: Policy Development (1-2 months)
- [ ] Customize policy template
- [ ] Legal review
- [ ] Insurance carrier review
- [ ] Administrative review and revisions
- [ ] Board approval (if required)

### Phase 3: Infrastructure (1 month)
- [ ] Designate operating areas
- [ ] Create charging station (fire-safe)
- [ ] Purchase safety equipment
- [ ] Set up storage
- [ ] Create documentation systems (forms, logs)

### Phase 4: Personnel Preparation
- [ ] Identify faculty operators
- [ ] Faculty obtain Part 107 certificates (allow 3-6 months)
- [ ] Conduct staff training
- [ ] Designate program coordinator

### Phase 5: Student Preparation
- [ ] Create student training program
- [ ] Prepare parent communication
- [ ] Distribute and collect consent forms
- [ ] Conduct student safety training

### Phase 6: Launch
- [ ] Pilot program with small group
- [ ] Document lessons learned
- [ ] Refine procedures
- [ ] Expand program gradually

### Phase 7: Ongoing
- [ ] Quarterly incident log review
- [ ] Semester equipment audit
- [ ] Annual policy review
- [ ] Annual insurance verification

## Common Policy Challenges

### Challenge 1: Student Use Without Part 107

**Issue:** Students under 16 can't get Part 107 certificate
**Solution:** Students operate under recreational exception (TRUST) with Part 107-certified adult maintaining visual line of sight and supervisory control

### Challenge 2: Insurance Concerns

**Issue:** Insurance carrier excludes or limits UAS coverage
**Solutions:**
- Shop for carrier with UAS coverage
- Add UAS rider to policy
- Operate only sub-250g platforms (lower risk profile)
- Implement strict safety policies to demonstrate risk management

### Challenge 3: Privacy Fears

**Issue:** Parents/community concerned about privacy
**Solutions:**
- Clear policy on camera use
- Transparent data handling procedures
- No flights over sensitive areas
- Community education sessions
- Opt-out provisions for privacy-concerned families

### Challenge 4: Competing with Personal Drones

**Issue:** Students want to fly personal drones on campus
**Solution:** Policy clearly states only approved school equipment on campus during school hours, personal drones violate policy

### Challenge 5: Faculty Without Part 107

**Issue:** Enthusiastic teachers lack certification
**Solutions:**
- Support faculty in obtaining Part 107 (study resources, test fees)
- Allow certified faculty to supervise multiple classrooms
- Start with sub-250g platforms requiring only TRUST
- Timeline for certification (6-12 months)
