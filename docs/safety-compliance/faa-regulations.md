# FAA Regulations for Unmanned Aircraft

!!! warning "USA-Specific Information"
    This page covers **United States Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) regulations only**. If you're operating outside the USA, research your country's aviation authority requirements.

## Overview

In the United States, the FAA regulates all use of the National Airspace System (NAS), including unmanned aircraft systems (UAS), commonly called drones. **All drone flights in the USA are subject to FAA regulations**, whether recreational, educational, or commercial.

## Key Definitions

**Unmanned Aircraft (UA):** An aircraft operated without a human pilot onboard

**Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS):** Includes the UA plus all associated equipment (controller, batteries, etc.)

**Small UAS:** UAS weighing less than 55 pounds (25 kg) - covers most consumer/educational drones

**Recreational Operation:** Flying purely for fun/personal enjoyment, no compensation

**Commercial Operation:** Flying for work, business, compensation, or in furtherance of a business (includes educational research in some cases)

## Two Primary Categories

### Recreational Flying (Part 44809 / "The Exception")

**Who this applies to:** Hobbyists, personal use, pure recreation

**Requirements:**

- [ ] **Pass TRUST test** - Free online aeronautical knowledge test (~30 minutes)
  - Available at: [www.faa.gov/uas/recreational_fliers/knowledge_test](https://www.faa.gov/uas/recreational_fliers/knowledge_test)
  - Required for all recreational flyers
  - Must carry proof of completion when flying

- [ ] **Register your drone** (if over 250 grams / 0.55 lbs)
  - Cost: $5 for 3 years
  - Register at: [faadronezone.faa.gov](https://faadronezone.faa.gov)
  - Mark registration number on aircraft
  - Carry registration certificate when flying

- [ ] **Follow CBO safety guidelines** (if operating under one)
  - CBO = Community-Based Organization (e.g., AMA - Academy of Model Aeronautics)
  - OR follow FAA's recreational flying guidelines

**Operational Rules:**

✅ **Must:**
- Fly only for recreational purposes
- Fly within visual line of sight (VLOS)
- Give way to manned aircraft
- Fly at or below 400 feet AGL (above ground level) in uncontrolled airspace
- Notify airport/air traffic control if within 5 miles of airport
- Never fly over people or moving vehicles (unless under cover or in limited circumstances)
- Never fly from moving vehicle (unless in rural area)
- Keep drone in sight at all times (no FPV-only flight without observer)

❌ **Must NOT:**
- Fly in controlled airspace without authorization (LAANC or airspace waiver)
- Fly at night without anti-collision lighting
- Fly while intoxicated
- Fly in emergency response areas (wildfires, disaster zones)
- Fly near stadiums, military bases, or other restricted areas
- Fly recklessly or endanger people/property

### Commercial/Educational Flying (Part 107)

**Who this applies to:**
- Real estate photography
- Inspection services
- Agriculture/surveying
- Educational research (often)
- ANY flight in furtherance of a business
- School-sanctioned activities (in many cases)

!!! question "Is my educational activity Part 107?"
    **Educational use is complex:**

    - **Student learning to fly for fun** → Recreational (Exception)
    - **School drone club** → Likely recreational
    - **Research data collection** → Likely Part 107
    - **Promotional video for school** → Part 107
    - **STEM competition with prize** → Likely Part 107

    **When in doubt, operate under Part 107 to be safe.**

**Requirements:**

- [ ] **Obtain Remote Pilot Certificate**
  - Must be at least 16 years old
  - Pass FAA Part 107 knowledge test ($175)
  - Test covers: regulations, airspace, weather, loading, operations
  - Study resources: FAA Part 107 guide, practice tests, online courses
  - Certificate valid for 24 months, then recurrent training required

- [ ] **Register drone** (same as recreational, $5)

**Operational Rules (Part 107):**

✅ **Allowed:**
- Operate during daylight or civil twilight (30 min before sunrise to 30 min after sunset)
- Operate in Class G airspace below 400 feet AGL without authorization
- Operate in controlled airspace with LAANC authorization or waiver
- Fly over people with certain categories of drones (see Part 107.39)

❌ **Prohibited without waiver:**
- Night operations (without anti-collision lighting and remote pilot training)
- Operations over people (unless drone meets Category 1-4 requirements)
- Operations from moving vehicle (over populated areas)
- Multiple drone operations by single pilot
- Careless or reckless operation

**Key Limitations:**
- Maximum groundspeed: 100 mph (87 knots)
- Maximum altitude: 400 feet AGL (or within 400 feet of structure if inspecting)
- Minimum visibility: 3 statute miles
- Minimum cloud clearances: 500 feet below, 2,000 feet horizontal
- Visual line of sight required (unless waived)

## Registration Requirements

### When Registration is Required

**Threshold: 250 grams (0.55 lbs)**

- **Under 250g:** No registration required (e.g., DJI Tello, Ryze Tello)
- **250g and over:** Registration required

!!! tip "Weigh Your Drone"
    Include battery and any payloads when weighing. A drone under 250g with battery but over with a camera requires registration.

### How to Register

1. **Create account** at [faadronezone.faa.gov](https://faadronezone.faa.gov)
2. **Provide information:**
   - Name, address, email
   - Recreational or Part 107
   - Credit card for $5 fee
3. **Receive registration number**
4. **Mark aircraft** with registration number
   - Must be visible without tools
   - Can be inside battery compartment if accessible
5. **Keep certificate** - Carry when flying (paper or digital)

**Registration valid:** 3 years

## Airspace Classification

Understanding airspace is critical for legal operations:

### Class G (Uncontrolled)
- **Most common** for recreational flying
- No ATC authorization required (up to 400 feet AGL)
- Still must follow all other rules

### Class E (Controlled)
- Controlled airspace, often around smaller airports
- LAANC authorization or waiver required
- Check airspace maps before flying

### Class D (Controlled)
- Around airports with control towers
- LAANC authorization or direct tower authorization required
- More restrictive

### Class C (Controlled)
- Around larger airports
- LAANC or waiver required
- Highly restrictive

### Class B (Controlled)
- Around major airports
- LAANC or waiver required
- Extremely restrictive, rarely approved

### Special Use Airspace
- **Restricted:** Military operations, hazardous activities - **DO NOT FLY**
- **Prohibited:** National security - **NEVER FLY** (e.g., DC area)
- **MOAs (Military Operating Areas):** Caution, military training
- **Temporary Flight Restrictions (TFRs):** Wildfires, VIP movement, stadiums

### Tools to Check Airspace

- **B4UFLY App:** FAA's official mobile app (iOS/Android)
- **Aloft:** Airspace awareness and LAANC
- **AirMap:** Airspace maps and LAANC
- **Kittyhawk:** Commercial operators

**ALWAYS check airspace before every flight location**

## LAANC (Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability)

**What it is:** Automated system for airspace authorization in controlled airspace

**How it works:**
1. Use LAANC-enabled app (Aloft, AirMap, etc.)
2. Enter flight location, altitude, time
3. Receive instant authorization (if approved) or need to request waiver
4. Keep authorization on phone/paper during flight

**Who can use it:**
- Recreational fliers (for controlled airspace)
- Part 107 pilots (most common use)

**Coverage:** Most controlled airspace around 700+ airports

**Cost:** Usually free (check provider)

## Special Restrictions

### Stadiums and Sporting Events
- **Prohibited:** Within 3 nautical miles, up to 3,000 feet AGL
- **Timing:** 1 hour before to 1 hour after event
- **Applies to:** NFL, MLB, NCAA Division 1, NASCAR, etc.

### Wildfires and Emergency Operations
- **TFRs issued immediately** for firefighting
- **Violating puts lives at risk** - firefighting aircraft grounded
- **Serious penalties** - up to $20,000 fine, criminal charges

### Washington DC Area
- **National Defense Airspace** - completely prohibited within 15-mile radius
- **Special Flight Rules Area** - additional restrictions outside that
- **Severe penalties** for violations

### Military Installations
- Restricted airspace around bases
- Usually no-fly zones
- Check NOTAMs and sectional charts

### National Parks
- **Prohibited by National Park Service** (not FAA)
- Applies to takeoff/landing/operation within park boundaries
- Check specific park regulations

## Penalties for Violations

The FAA takes violations seriously:

### Civil Penalties
- **Fines up to $27,500** per violation
- **License suspension** for certificated pilots
- **Registration revocation**

### Criminal Penalties (Reckless Operation)
- **Fines up to $250,000**
- **Imprisonment up to 3 years**
- **Felony record**

### Examples of Violations Leading to Penalties
- Flying in restricted airspace (DC area, airports)
- Interfering with manned aircraft
- Flying over stadiums during events
- Reckless operation endangering people/property

**The FAA has enforcement authority and actively investigates reports.**

## Educational Institutions - Special Considerations

### Schools and Universities

**Part 107 vs. Recreational:**
- Pure student learning/recreation → Recreational (Exception)
- Research, data collection, promotional use → Part 107
- Competitions with prizes/recognition → Likely Part 107

**Recommendations for Schools:**
1. **Designate a responsible person** to obtain Part 107 certificate
2. **Create institutional policy** (see [School Policies](school-policies.md))
3. **Train all operators** regardless of which rules apply
4. **Maintain logs** of all flights
5. **Obtain insurance** - liability coverage
6. **Create written procedures** for authorization and safety

### Student Operations

**Under 16 years old:**
- Cannot obtain Part 107 certificate
- Can fly recreationally under supervision
- Adult with Part 107 must supervise if commercial/research use

**16+ years old:**
- Can obtain Part 107 certificate
- Can operate for educational research independently (with school approval)

## Quick Reference Checklist

### Before Every Flight

- [ ] Airspace checked (B4UFLY or equivalent)
- [ ] LAANC authorization obtained (if needed)
- [ ] Weather acceptable
- [ ] Registration current and on-person
- [ ] TRUST completion certificate (recreational) or Part 107 certificate on-person
- [ ] No TFRs in effect
- [ ] Not within 5 miles of airport OR airport/tower notified
- [ ] No stadium events nearby

## Common Questions

??? question "Do I need Part 107 for my school project?"

    **It depends:**

    - **Purely learning to fly?** No, recreational rules apply
    - **Data collection for research?** Likely yes
    - **Taking photos for yearbook/website?** Yes (promotional use)
    - **Competition with prize?** Probably yes

    **When uncertain, operate under Part 107 to be compliant.**

??? question "Can a 14-year-old student fly drones at school?"

    **Recreationally:** Yes, with proper supervision and TRUST completion

    **For school purposes (Part 107 activity):** Student can operate under the direct supervision of a Part 107-certified adult who maintains VLOS

??? question "What if my drone is under 250g?"

    - No registration required
    - Still must follow all operational rules (airspace, altitude, etc.)
    - TRUST still required for recreational
    - Part 107 still required if commercial/educational use

??? question "Can I fly at night?"

    **Recreational:** Yes, if drone has anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles

    **Part 107:** Yes, with anti-collision lighting AND completion of Part 107 night operations training (free online recurrent training)
