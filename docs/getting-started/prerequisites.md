# Prerequisites

Before diving into unmanned vehicle systems, it's important to understand what you'll need. This guide covers knowledge, skills, tools, budget, and legal requirements.

## Knowledge & Skills Assessment

### Level 1: Complete Beginner (RTF Platforms)

**Required Knowledge:**

- ✅ Basic understanding of batteries and charging
- ✅ Ability to follow instructions carefully
- ✅ Understanding of basic safety concepts

**Optional but Helpful:**

- 📘 Basic physics (gravity, momentum, forces)
- 📘 Familiarity with remote controls (RC cars, video games)

**Time to Get Started:** 1-2 hours of safety reading

---

### Level 2: Hobby Experience (BNF Platforms)

**Required Knowledge:**

- ✅ Everything from Level 1
- ✅ Understanding of radio control systems (transmitter/receiver)
- ✅ Basic electronics concepts (voltage, current, polarity)
- ✅ Ability to use basic hand tools

**Optional but Helpful:**

- 📘 Experience with RC cars, boats, or planes
- 📘 Reading of wiring diagrams
- 📘 Basic computer skills for configuration software

**Time to Get Started:** 4-6 hours of learning and setup

---

### Level 3: Maker/Hobbyist (ARF Platforms)

**Required Knowledge:**

- ✅ Everything from Levels 1-2
- ✅ Soldering skills (clean joints, heat control)
- ✅ Reading electronic schematics
- ✅ Multimeter usage (voltage, continuity testing)
- ✅ Basic troubleshooting methodology
- ✅ Understanding of ESCs, motors, and flight controllers

**Optional but Helpful:**

- 📘 Programming basics (for configuration)
- 📘 CAD software for custom parts
- 📘 3D printing knowledge
- 📘 Basic PID tuning concepts

**Time to Get Started:** 2-3 weeks of building and learning

---

### Level 4: Advanced/Engineering (Custom Builds)

**Required Knowledge:**

- ✅ Everything from Levels 1-3
- ✅ Programming (Python, C++, or similar)
- ✅ Control systems fundamentals (PID, state machines)
- ✅ CAD design for custom components
- ✅ Advanced electronics (power systems, signal processing)
- ✅ Debugging complex systems
- ✅ Reading datasheets and technical documentation

**Strongly Recommended:**

- 📘 Linear algebra and calculus (for advanced control)
- 📘 Understanding of filters (Kalman, complementary)
- 📘 Network protocols (MAVLink, ROS)
- 📘 Version control (Git)
- 📘 Sensor fusion concepts

**Time to Get Started:** 1-3 months for first complete build

| **Level 4 (Custom)** | $400-1,200 | $782-1,762 | $200 | $1,382-3,162 |

### Recurring Costs (Annual Estimates)

- **Batteries:** $50-150 (replacements as they degrade)
- **Propellers/parts:** $30-100 (crashes and wear)
- **Upgrades:** $0-500 (optional improvements)
- **Consumables:** $20-50 (solder, glue, tape, etc.)

**Annual: $100-800 depending on usage**

!!! tip "Money-Saving Strategies"

    - **Buy in bulk:** Propellers, batteries, connectors
    - **Join group orders:** Split shipping with friends/classmates
    - **Use simulators:** Practice flying/driving in simulation first (free, no crashes)
    - **Buy used:** Check forums for used platforms (inspect carefully)
    - **Start small:** Master cheap platform before expensive one
    - **DIY repairs:** Learn to fix instead of replace

## Required Safety Equipment

!!! danger "Non-Negotiable Safety Items"

    These are **required** before operating any unmanned vehicle:

### Fire Safety (for LiPo batteries)

- [ ] **LiPo safe bag** (fireproof charging/storage bag) - $10-20
- [ ] **Fire extinguisher** (ABC or CO2 type) - $20-40
- [ ] **Metal container or ammo box** (for long-term storage) - $15-30
- [ ] **Smoke detector** (in charging area) - $15-25

**Why:** LiPo batteries can catch fire if damaged, overcharged, or mishandled. See [Battery Safety](../safety-compliance/battery-safety.md).

### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

- [ ] **Safety glasses** (for building, flying near you) - $10-20
- [ ] **First aid kit** (for minor cuts, burns) - $15-30
- [ ] **Heat-resistant mat** (for soldering) - $10-15
- [ ] **Well-ventilated workspace** (for soldering fumes) - Free

### Field Equipment

- [ ] **High-visibility vest** (if operating outdoors) - $10-15
- [ ] **Sunscreen & hat** (outdoor operations) - $10-20
- [ ] **Radio communication** (if flying with spotter) - $30-60

**Total Safety Investment: $125-250**

## Legal & Regulatory Requirements

### United States (FAA)

**Recreational Flyers:**

- [ ] Complete TRUST test (free, online, ~30 minutes)
- [ ] Register drone if **>250 grams** ($5, valid 3 years)
- [ ] Follow recreational flying rules:
  - Fly at or below 400 feet AGL
  - Keep aircraft in visual line of sight
  - Never fly near other aircraft
  - Notify airports/control towers when within 5 miles
  - Don't fly over people or moving vehicles

**Educational/Commercial:**

- [ ] Obtain Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate (~$175 test fee)
- [ ] Pass aeronautical knowledge test
- [ ] Different operational rules apply

**More:** See [FAA Regulations](../safety-compliance/faa-regulations.md)

---

### Age Requirements

**Minimum ages by regulation:**

- **FAA TRUST:** No age limit (parental supervision recommended <13)
- **FAA Part 107:** 16 years old
- **RC flying fields:** Typically 13+ (varies by location)

**Recommended:**

- **Under 12:** Direct adult supervision at all times
- **12-16:** Adult present, progressive independence
- **16+:** Can operate independently (with proper training)

## Workspace Requirements

### Minimum Workspace

- **Desk/table:** 4' × 2' clear workspace
- **Lighting:** Good overhead lighting or desk lamp
- **Ventilation:** Window or fan (for soldering)
- **Power:** Multiple outlets, surge protector
- **Storage:** Shelves or bins for parts organization

### Ideal Workspace Additions

- **Tool organizer:** Keep tools accessible
- **Parts bins:** Small drawers for screws, connectors
- **Magnetic parts tray:** Hold small metal parts during assembly
- **Whiteboard:** Sketch ideas, track projects
- **Computer/monitor:** Reference documentation while building

### Flying/Operating Space

**Indoor (Micro drones/rovers):**

- Minimum 10' × 10' clear space
- High ceiling (8'+)
- No breakable items nearby
- Good lighting

**Outdoor (Standard platforms):**

- Open field, 50' × 50' minimum
- No people, animals, or vehicles nearby
- Clear of trees and power lines
- Legal flying area (check local laws)

## Skills Development Resources

### Before You Buy

**Recommended preparation:**

1. **Watch tutorial videos:** YouTube channels (Joshua Bardwell, Painless360, RCModelReviews)
2. **Use flight simulators:** Practice flying without risk ($20-25)
3. **Read documentation:** ArduPilot, PX4, Betaflight wikis
4. **Join online communities:** Reddit, RCGroups, Discord servers
5. **Visit local RC clubs:** Hands-on experience, mentorship

### Learning Timeline Estimates

**To first flight/drive:**

- **Level 1 (RTF):** 1-2 hours
- **Level 2 (BNF):** 1-3 days
- **Level 3 (ARF):** 1-3 weeks
- **Level 4 (Custom):** 1-3 months

**To confident operation:**

- **Level 1:** 5-10 hours of practice
- **Level 2:** 15-25 hours
- **Level 3:** 30-50 hours
- **Level 4:** 50-100+ hours
