# Mentorship Program Overview

The Unmanned Vehicle Systems Education Hub Mentorship Program connects experienced educators with those new to unmanned systems, providing structured support, guidance, and community.

## Program Mission

To accelerate educator success through personalized mentorship, reducing the isolation and uncertainty that comes with pioneering new educational programs.

## Why Mentorship?

Starting an unmanned vehicle systems program can be overwhelming. A mentor provides:

- **Experience**: Learn from someone who's been there
- **Guidance**: Navigate challenges with support
- **Confidence**: Build skills with expert backing
- **Network**: Connect to broader community
- **Accountability**: Stay on track with regular check-ins
- **Problem-solving**: Troubleshoot issues together
- **Encouragement**: Celebrate successes, learn from setbacks

## Program Structure

### Time Commitment

**Mentors**: 2-4 hours per month
- 1-hour monthly meeting with mentee
- Async communication between meetings
- Preparation time
- Optional: Participation in mentor community

**Mentees**: 2-3 hours per month
- 1-hour monthly meeting with mentor
- Follow-through on action items
- Async communication as needed
- Reflection and documentation

### Duration

**Standard program**: 6 months (one academic semester)
- Initial matching and onboarding
- 5 monthly check-in meetings
- Final review and transition planning

**Extended program**: 12 months (full academic year)
- For mentees implementing full curriculum
- Covers planning, launch, and refinement phases

**Renewable**: Pairs can continue beyond initial period with mutual agreement

### Meeting Structure

**Monthly meetings** follow structured agendas:

1. **First Meeting** ([agenda template](meeting-agenda-templates/first-meeting-agenda.md))
   - Get to know each other
   - Set goals and expectations
   - Establish communication preferences
   - Plan first implementation steps

2. **Monthly Check-ins** ([agenda template](meeting-agenda-templates/monthly-check-in-agenda.md))
   - Review progress since last meeting
   - Discuss challenges and successes
   - Problem-solve current issues
   - Set action items for next month

3. **Quarterly Reviews** ([agenda template](meeting-agenda-templates/quarterly-review-agenda.md))
   - Assess progress toward goals
   - Celebrate achievements
   - Adjust goals if needed
   - Plan for next quarter

## Who Should Apply

### Ideal Mentees

You're a great fit if you:

- Are new to unmanned vehicle systems education (0-2 years)

- Want structured support and guidance
- Can commit to monthly meetings
- Are willing to implement and report progress
- Want to accelerate your learning

**Common mentee profiles:**

- Experienced teacher new to this technology
- Starting a competition team
- Developing new curriculum

### Ideal Mentors

You're a great fit if you:

- Have 2+ years successfully teaching unmanned systems
- Enjoy helping others grow
- Can commit to regular meetings
- Are patient and encouraging
- Have overcome common challenges
- Want to give back to community

**Mentor qualifications:**
- Successfully implemented unmanned systems curriculum
- Positive student outcomes and engagement
- Strong troubleshooting and problem-solving skills
- Effective communication skills
- Growth mindset and continuous learner
- Commitment to inclusive education

## Matching Process

### How Matching Works

1. **Applications** - Both mentors and mentees submit applications
2. **Review** - Program coordinators review applications
3. **Matching** - Pairs are matched based on:
   - Teaching context (grade level, subject area)
   - Technical focus (UAV, UGV, both)
   - Geographic location (optional, for in-person connection)
   - Specific goals and expertise
   - Personality and teaching philosophy
   - Schedule compatibility
4. **Introduction** - Matched pairs are introduced via email
5. **First Meeting** - Pair schedules initial meeting

### Matching Timeline

**Rolling admissions**: Applications accepted year-round

**Matching cohorts**:
- **Summer cohort** (June-August) - Planning phase mentorship
- **Fall cohort** (September-December) - Semester-long implementation
- **Spring cohort** (January-May) - Spring semester implementation
- **Year-long cohort** (September-June) - Full academic year

### What We Consider in Matching

**For mentees:**
- Experience level and needs
- Implementation timeline
- Specific challenges or goals
- Teaching context
- Learning style preferences

**For mentors:**
- Areas of expertise
- Teaching context experience
- Availability and time zones
- Mentoring style and strengths
- Language preferences

**For compatibility:**
- Communication preferences (async vs. sync heavy)
- Problem-solving approaches
- Program philosophy alignment
- Mutual interests beyond unmanned systems

## Program Components

### One-on-One Meetings

Monthly video calls (or in-person if local):
- Structured agendas provided
- Focus on mentee's current needs
- Mix of support, problem-solving, and planning
- Documented action items

### Asynchronous Support

Between meetings:
- Email or messaging for quick questions
- Resource sharing
- Troubleshooting assistance
- Encouragement and check-ins

Mentors are not expected to be available 24/7, but typically respond within 48 hours.

### Mentor Community

Mentors have access to:
- Private mentor discussion group
- Monthly mentor office hours
- Mentor training resources
- Peer support from other mentors

### Mentee Community

Mentees have access to:
- Mentee cohort discussions
- Group workshops on common topics
- Peer connections with other mentees
- Broader community resources

### Documentation

Participants document their experience:
- Meeting notes (using templates)
- Progress tracking
- Reflection journals
- Success stories and challenges

This helps:
- Track progress toward goals
- Identify patterns and insights
- Contribute to program improvement
- Share learnings with community (anonymously)

## Special Situations

### Re-matching

If a match isn't working:
1. Both parties discuss concerns openly
2. Involve program coordinator if needed
3. Re-matching arranged if necessary
4. No penalty or judgment

Common re-match reasons:
- Different expectations or communication styles
- Life circumstances changed
- Better match identified
- Personality incompatibility

### Mentor Leave

If mentor needs to pause:
- Notify mentee and coordinator ASAP
- Arrange temporary or permanent replacement
- Ensure smooth transition for mentee

### Extended Mentorship

Want to continue beyond 6 months?
- Discuss with your partner
- Complete renewal form
- May transition to peer relationship instead

### Group Mentoring

Some mentors work with 2-3 mentees:
- Group meetings for common topics
- Individual meetings for specific needs
- Peer learning among mentees
- Requires strong facilitation

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if my mentor doesn't know the answer to my question?**
A: That's normal! Mentors help you find answers and troubleshoot together. They're guides, not omniscient experts.

**Q: Can I have more than one mentor?**
A: Yes, in some cases. Discuss with program coordinator. Some people have a primary mentor plus informal mentors for specific topics.

**Q: What if I'm too busy for monthly meetings?**
A: Mentorship requires consistent engagement. If you can't commit to monthly meetings, wait until you have capacity. Quality > quantity.

**Q: Can I be both a mentor and mentee?**
A: Absolutely! You might mentor someone earlier in their journey while being mentored by someone more experienced.

**Q: What if my situation changes mid-program?**
A: Life happens. Communicate with your partner and coordinator. We can adjust, pause, or transition as needed.

**Q: Is mentorship only for classroom teachers?**
A: No! We support club advisors, homeschool parents, community educators, makerspace facilitators, and more.

**Q: What if I'm not near any other participants?**
A: Most mentorship is virtual. Geographic matching is optional but not required.

**Q: How do I know if I'm ready to be a mentor?**
A: If you've successfully run a program for 2+ years and enjoy helping others, you're likely ready. Mentors don't need to know everything!
