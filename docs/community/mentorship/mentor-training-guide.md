# Mentor Training Guide

**Required Training**: 2-hour session (live or recorded)

## Training Objectives

By the end of training, mentors will be able to:
- Understand effective mentoring principles
- Use program templates and resources
- Set appropriate boundaries
- Support diverse mentees
- Navigate common challenges
- Know when and how to escalate issues

## Module 1: Effective Mentoring (30 min)

### What Makes a Good Mentor?

**Key qualities:**
- **Listening > Telling** - Ask questions, don't lecture
- **Guide, don't rescue** - Help mentees solve problems themselves
- **Share failures too** - Vulnerability builds trust
- **Celebrate progress** - Acknowledge growth
- **Set boundaries** - Protect your time and energy
- **Stay learner-focused** - About their growth, not your ego

### Mentoring vs. Teaching

**Teaching**: Knowledge transfer, directive, structured
**Mentoring**: Growth facilitation, collaborative, responsive

**Mentors don't need to:**
- Know everything
- Solve every problem
- Be available 24/7
- Do the work for mentee

**Mentors should:**
- Listen actively
- Ask powerful questions
- Share experiences
- Connect to resources
- Provide accountability
- Offer encouragement

### Powerful Questions

Instead of telling, ask:
- "What have you tried so far?"
- "What do you think might work?"
- "What's holding you back?"
- "What would success look like?"
- "What do you need to move forward?"
- "What can you learn from this setback?"

## Module 2: Program Structure (20 min)

### Using Meeting Templates
- First meeting sets foundation
- Monthly check-ins maintain momentum
- Quarterly reviews assess progress

### Documentation Expectations
- Brief meeting notes
- Track action items
- Note mentee's growth
- Share success stories (anonymously)

### Communication Guidelines
- Respond within 48 hours (weekdays)
- Set clear availability
- Use async communication effectively
- Know when to schedule extra meeting

## Module 3: Supporting Diverse Mentees (25 min)

### Cultural Competency
- Recognize your own biases
- Avoid assumptions
- Ask about context
- Respect different approaches

### Diverse Teaching Contexts
- Resource constraints
- Rural vs. urban settings
- Different student populations
- Varying administrative support

### Accessibility Considerations
- Diverse learning styles
- Different communication preferences
- Various technical skill levels
- Accommodating disabilities

### Meeting Mentees Where They Are
- Beginners need different support than experienced educators
- Confidence varies widely
- Some need technical help, others pedagogical
- Adjust your approach accordingly

## Module 4: Common Challenges (25 min)

### Challenge: Mentee is overwhelmed
**Response:** Break goals into smaller steps, celebrate small wins, adjust expectations

### Challenge: Mentee isn't following through
**Response:** Explore barriers, adjust workload, check motivation, revisit goals

### Challenge: Technical problem beyond your expertise
**Response:** Say "I don't know but let's figure it out together," connect to expert, learn together

### Challenge: Mentee wants you to solve everything
**Response:** Ask guiding questions, build their problem-solving skills, resist rescue urge

### Challenge: Communication is difficult
**Response:** Discuss preferences directly, try different methods, involve coordinator if needed

### Challenge: You don't have time
**Response:** Be honest immediately, adjust commitment, or gracefully transition mentee

## Module 5: Boundaries and Ethics (15 min)

### Appropriate Boundaries

**DO:**
- Maintain professional relationship
- Keep focus on mentee's growth
- Respect confidentiality
- Set clear availability
- Share professional resources

**DON'T:**
- Share inappropriate personal details
- Develop romantic/personal relationship
- Gossip about mentee
- Overextend yourself
- Solve problems they should tackle

### Confidentiality
- What mentee shares stays private
- Exception: Safety concerns
- Can share anonymized successes
- Don't discuss with other community members

### When to Escalate
Contact program coordinator if:
- Safety concerns (student or educator)
- Ethical violations
- Match isn't working
- Mentee needs specialized support
- You need to withdraw from program
- Mental health concerns
