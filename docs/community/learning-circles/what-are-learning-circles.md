# What Are Learning Circles?

Learning circles are small, peer-led study groups where educators collaboratively explore topics, solve problems, and support each other's growth in unmanned vehicle systems education.

## Overview

**Definition:** A learning circle is a facilitated study group of 4-8 educators who meet regularly (weekly or biweekly) to learn together through structured activities and peer support.

**Duration:** Typically 6-12 weeks per circle, focused on a specific topic or goal

**Format:** Virtual (video call) or in-person, 60-90 minutes per session

## Key Characteristics

### Peer-Led
- Not instructor-led teaching
- All members contribute and learn
- Facilitator guides process, not content expert
- Democratic and collaborative

### Goal-Oriented
- Specific topic or objective
- Clear outcomes defined
- Structured learning path
- Measurable progress

### Accountable
- Regular meeting commitment
- Between-session work
- Peer accountability
- Shared responsibility

### Supportive
- Safe space to struggle
- Celebrate successes together
- Troubleshoot challenges collectively
- Build lasting connections

## Types of Learning Circles

### Technical Skills Circles
**Focus:** Building specific technical competencies
**Examples:**
- Mastering BetaFlight configuration
- Python programming for drones
- PID tuning workshop
- Soldering and electronics

### Book Study Circles
**Focus:** Deep engagement with educational resources
**Examples:**
- Reading and discussing drone-related books
- Exploring pedagogical theory
- Reviewing research papers
- Analyzing case studies

### Lesson Plan Development Circles
**Focus:** Collaborative curriculum creation
**Examples:**
- Developing unit plans together
- Creating assessment tools
- Designing project-based learning
- Aligning to standards

### Troubleshooting Circles
**Focus:** Solving common technical problems
**Examples:**
- Weekly problem-solving sessions
- Bring-your-own-problem format
- Systematic debugging practice
- Building troubleshooting skills

### Implementation Circles
**Focus:** Supporting program launch or expansion
**Examples:**
- First-year educators cohort
- Scaling up programs
- Adding new platforms
- Starting competition teams

### Research/Inquiry Circles
**Focus:** Investigating questions together
**Examples:**
- Action research projects
- Exploring best practices
- Testing teaching strategies
- Evaluating student outcomes

## Learning Circle Structure

### Typical Meeting Format

**Opening (10 min):**
- Check-in
- Celebrate wins
- Review agenda
- Set intentions

**Core Activity (40-60 min):**
- Depends on circle type
- Structured but flexible
- Active participation
- Hands-on when possible

**Application Planning (10-15 min):**
- How will you use this?
- What will you try in your classroom?
- Action items for next week
- Resource needs

**Closing (5-10 min):**
- Key takeaways
- Appreciations
- Preview next session
- Logistics

### Between Sessions

**Individual work:**
- Reading or preparation
- Trying strategies in classroom
- Completing assignments
- Gathering materials

**Async collaboration:**
- Shared documents or notes
- Discussion forum
- Resource sharing
- Question posting

## Success Factors

**Clear Focus:**
- Specific topic, not too broad
- Defined outcomes
- Appropriate duration
- Right level for participants

**Right Size:**
- 4-8 participants ideal
- Enough for diverse perspectives
- Small enough for participation
- Can split if too large

**Committed Members:**
- Regular attendance
- Between-session work
- Active participation
- Mutual accountability

**Effective Facilitation:**
- Organized but flexible
- Inclusive environment
- Keeps group on track
- Manages dynamics

**Structured Flexibility:**
- Clear format and expectations
- Room for emergent needs
- Responsive to group
- Balance structure and flow

## Common Challenges

**Challenge: Low attendance**
**Solutions:** Clear expectations upfront, flexible scheduling, meaningful content, accountability buddies

**Challenge: Unequal participation**
**Solutions:** Structured turn-taking, breakout pairs, pre-work requirements, facilitator invitations

**Challenge: Off-track discussions**
**Solutions:** Parking lot for tangents, gentle redirecting, clear agenda, time boxes

**Challenge: Technical difficulties**
**Solutions:** Tech check before first meeting, backup platform, recorded sessions, async options

**Challenge: Maintaining momentum**
**Solutions:** Clear schedule, between-session engagement, visible progress, celebration of wins

## FAQs

**Q: How long do learning circles last?**
A: Typically 6-12 weeks, meeting weekly or biweekly. Some continue longer by group choice.

**Q: Do I need to be an expert to join or facilitate?**
A: No! Learning circles are peer learning. Facilitators guide process, not content.

**Q: What if I can't attend every session?**
A: Communicate with your group. Missing 1-2 sessions is usually okay. Regular attendance is expected.

**Q: Are learning circles formal professional development?**
A: Some count for PD credit (check with your institution). All provide valuable learning.

**Q: Can students participate in learning circles?**
A: Student learning circles are encouraged! See [Student Community](../../../resources/student-community/peer-learning/) for student-focused options.

**Q: What's the difference between a learning circle and a mentorship?**
A: Mentorship is 1-on-1 with an experienced mentor. Learning circles are peer-based group learning.

**Q: Do learning circles cost anything?**
A: No! All community learning circles are free.
