# Starting a Learning Circle

A step-by-step guide to launching your own learning circle.

## Before You Start

**Ask yourself:**
- What topic am I passionate about learning?
- Who else might be interested in this?
- Can I commit to facilitating 6-12 weeks?
- Do I have time to organize this?

**You don't need to be an expert!** You need to be:
- Interested in the topic
- Willing to facilitate
- Organized and reliable
- Committed to the duration

## Step-by-Step Launch Process

### Step 1: Define Your Circle (Week 1)

**Choose your topic:**
- Specific enough to be focused
- Broad enough to sustain 6+ weeks
- Matched to a real need
- Interesting to you and others

**Examples:**
- "Mastering BetaFlight CLI Commands" (technical)
- "Implementing Project-Based Learning with Drones" (pedagogical)
- "Building Competition-Ready Robots" (hands-on)
- "Reading: Teaching in the Outdoors" (book study)

**Define learning outcomes:**
What will participants be able to do by the end?

**Determine format:**
- [ ] Technical workshop
- [ ] Book club
- [ ] Lesson study
- [ ] Troubleshooting
- [ ] Project-based
- [ ] Other: _______________

**Set logistics:**
- **Duration:** ___ weeks
- **Meeting frequency:** Weekly / Biweekly
- **Meeting length:** 60 / 90 minutes
- **Format:** Virtual / In-person / Hybrid
- **Platform:** Zoom / Google Meet / Other
- **Day/Time:** _______________

### Step 2: Plan Your Sessions (Week 1-2)

**Create rough outline** for each session:

**Week 1:** Introduction and foundations
**Week 2:** _______________
**Week 3:** _______________
**Week 4:** _______________
**Week 5:** _______________
**Week 6:** _______________
**Week 7-12:** _______________

**For each session, consider:**
- Core topic or activity
- Required preparation
- Materials needed
- Expected outcomes
- Application to teaching

**Use meeting format templates:**
- [Lesson Study](meeting-formats/lesson-study-format.md)
- [Book Club](meeting-formats/book-club-format.md)
- [Technical Workshop](meeting-formats/technical-workshop-format.md)
- [Troubleshooting](meeting-formats/peer-troubleshooting-format.md)

### Step 3: Recruit Participants (Week 2-3)

**Create recruitment post** including:
- Circle topic and goals
- Format and duration
- Meeting logistics
- Prerequisites (if any)
- Expected commitment
- How to join

**Post in:**
- [GitHub Discussions - Learning Circles](https://github.com/F3DESIGNED/unmanned-vehicle-edu-hub/discussions)
- Community announcements
- Regional chapter forums
- Related discussion threads

**Sample recruitment post:**
```
🔵 NEW LEARNING CIRCLE: Mastering PID Tuning

Join us for a 6-week technical workshop where we'll dive deep into
PID tuning for multirotors. We'll learn theory, practice with
simulators, and tune real quads.

📅 Wednesdays, 7-8:30 PM ET, starting March 5
🎥 Virtual (Zoom)
👥 Limited to 8 participants
✅ Prerequisites: Basic BetaFlight experience, access to quad or sim

By the end, you'll be able to tune a quad from scratch and teach
PID fundamentals to your students.

Interested? Comment below or message me!
```

**Target recruitment:**
- 6-10 interested (expecting some drop-off)
- Aim for 4-8 active participants
- Screen for commitment and fit
- Create waiting list if oversubscribed

**Complete registration** using [Circle Registration Template](circle-registration-template.md)

**Submit to:** community-learning-circles@[placeholder] or [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/F3DESIGNED/unmanned-vehicle-edu-hub/discussions)

**Benefits of registering:**

- Community support

### Step 5: Onboard Participants (Week 4)

**Hold orientation meeting** (30-45 min):
- Introductions
- Review goals and format
- Set expectations
- Establish norms
- Share schedule
- Test technology
- Answer questions

**Establish group norms:**
- Attendance expectations
- Communication channels
- Between-session work
- Participation guidelines
- Confidentiality
- Problem-solving approach

**Set up infrastructure:**
- [ ] Shared calendar with all dates
- [ ] Communication channel (email list, Slack, etc.)
- [ ] Shared document space (Google Drive, etc.)
- [ ] Video meeting links
- [ ] Resource repository

**Send welcome packet:**
- Meeting schedule with links
- Communication protocols
- Reading/prep for Week 1
- Materials needed
- Facilitator contact info
- Group member list (with permission)

### Step 6: Facilitate Sessions (Weeks 5+)

**Before each session:**
- [ ] Review planned agenda
- [ ] Prepare materials
- [ ] Send reminder and prep work (2-3 days before)
- [ ] Test technology
- [ ] Gather any needed resources

**During each session:**
- [ ] Start on time
- [ ] Welcome everyone
- [ ] Follow your format
- [ ] Manage time
- [ ] Ensure participation
- [ ] Capture key points
- [ ] Assign next prep
- [ ] End on time

**After each session:**
- [ ] Share notes or recording
- [ ] Follow up on questions
- [ ] Prepare for next week
- [ ] Check in with quiet members
- [ ] Adjust plans if needed

**See:** [Circle Facilitator Guide](circle-facilitator-guide.md) for detailed facilitation strategies

### Step 7: Celebrate and Close (Final week)

**Final session activities:**
- Review learning journey
- Celebrate achievements
- Share what participants will do next
- Gather feedback
- Exchange appreciations
- Discuss continuing or future circles
- Take group photo (optional)

**Close-out tasks:**
- [ ] Send final summary
- [ ] Share all resources
- [ ] Complete facilitator reflection
- [ ] Submit completion report
- [ ] Recognize participants
- [ ] Archive materials for future use

### Step 8: Reflect and Share (Post-circle)

**Personal reflection:**
- What worked well?
- What would you do differently?
- What did you learn as facilitator?
- Would you facilitate again?

**Share with community:**
- Write brief summary of circle outcomes
- Share resources created
- Post in [Show & Tell](https://github.com/F3DESIGNED/unmanned-vehicle-edu-hub/discussions)
- Offer to advise future facilitators
- Consider running another circle!

## FAQs for New Facilitators

**Q: What if I'm not an expert in the topic?**
A: Perfect! Learning circles are about learning together. Your job is to facilitate, not teach.

**Q: How much time does facilitating require?**
A: Expect 2-3 hours/week: 1-1.5 hr meeting + 1-1.5 hr prep/follow-up.

**Q: What if people drop out?**
A: Common! Start with 6-8. If you drop below 4, consider merging with another circle or continuing with smaller group.

**Q: What if I can't facilitate one week?**
A: Ask a participant to co-facilitate, pre-record, cancel that week, or invite a guest. Communicate early!

**Q: What if the group wants to go in a different direction?**
A: Great! Be flexible. Adjust to group needs while maintaining focus.

**Q: Can I get co-facilitators?**
A: Absolutely! Co-facilitation is encouraged, especially for first-timers.

**Q: What if someone dominates discussions?**
A: Use structured turn-taking, breakout pairs, and private conversation if needed.

**Q: Is this a lot of work?**
A: Moderate effort, high reward. Good planning upfront makes facilitation smoother.
