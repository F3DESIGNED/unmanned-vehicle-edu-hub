## For Educators

### Getting Started

**Assess your current accessibility:**
1. Review classroom physical accessibility
2. Audit curriculum materials (WCAG check)
3. Survey students about needs
4. Identify barriers
5. Plan improvements

**Prioritize high-impact changes:**
- Captions on all videos (helps everyone)
- Multiple ways to demonstrate learning
- Flexible seating and grouping
- Clear, consistent organization
- Student choice in assignments

### Proactive Measures

**Build accessibility in from start:**
- Design lessons with UDL principles
- Create materials to WCAG standards
- Offer multiple formats
- Plan for various learning styles
- Build in flexibility

**Better than retroactive accommodations!**

### Working with Support Services

**Collaborate with:**
- Special education teachers
- ELL specialists
- Occupational therapists
- Speech-language pathologists
- Assistive technology specialists
- School counselors

**Together you can:**
- Modify materials
- Adapt activities
- Select assistive tech
- Design assessments
- Support inclusion

## For Content Creators

**When creating resources:**

✅ **Digital Content:**
- Add alt text to images
- Caption all videos
- Use semantic HTML
- High-contrast text
- Keyboard navigable
- Screen reader compatible

✅ **Physical Materials:**
- Large, clear fonts (14pt+)
- Simple language
- Visual supports
- Multiple formats
- Clear organization

✅ **Activities:**
- Multiple entry points
- Flexible grouping
- Various difficulty levels
- Choice in demonstration
- Scaffolded support

## Philosophy

**We believe:**
- Disability is part of human diversity
- Barriers are in environment, not person
- Accessibility benefits everyone
- Inclusion requires intentional design
- High expectations + appropriate support
- Student voice and choice matter

**We reject:**
- "This field isn't for everyone"
- Gatekeeping based on ability
- One-size-fits-all approaches
- Retrofitting accessibility as afterthought
- Lowered expectations
