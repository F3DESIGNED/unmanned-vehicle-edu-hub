# Publishing Student Research

A guide to disseminating undergraduate research through publications, presentations, and competitions.

## Why Publish Student Research?

### Benefits for Students
- **Resume builder**: Demonstrates research capability
- **Graduate school**: Essential for competitive programs
- **Scholarships**: Many require research experience
- **Confidence**: Validation of work quality
- **Network**: Connect with researchers
- **Impact**: Contribute to field

### Benefits for Programs
- Demonstrates program quality
- Attracts strong students
- Builds partnerships
- Gains visibility
- Supports funding applications

## Publication Venues

### Undergraduate-Specific Journals

**Council on Undergraduate Research (CUR)**
- **Focus**: All STEM disciplines
- **Level**: Accessible to undergrads
- **Review**: Educational, developmental feedback
- **Timeline**: 2-4 months
- **Cost**: Free

**Journal of Purdue Undergraduate Research**
- Established undergraduate journal
- Rigorous but supportive review
- Free and open access
- Good for first publication

**Undergraduate Research Journals by Field**
- Check if robotics/engineering journals accept undergraduate work
- Often have special sections or issues

### Conferences

**National Conference on Undergraduate Research (NCUR)**
- Annual conference specifically for undergrads
- Oral presentations and posters
- All disciplines
- Highly supportive environment
- Good first conference experience

**Regional Undergraduate Research Conferences**
- Lower cost and travel
- Less competitive
- Good practice for national conferences

**Major Robotics Conferences (competitive)**
- **ICRA** (International Conference on Robotics and Automation)
  - Workshop papers more accessible
  - Poster sessions
  - Student paper competitions

- **IROS** (Intelligent Robots and Systems)
  - Similar to ICRA
  - Student activities
  - Late-breaking results posters

- **RSS** (Robotics: Science and Systems)
  - Smaller, more selective
  - Strong theoretical focus

- **HRI** (Human-Robot Interaction)
  - If project involves HRI
  - Student design competition

**Domain-Specific Conferences**
- SUAS (Student Unmanned Aerial Systems Competition)
- AUVSI XPONENTIAL
- Computer vision conferences (CVPR, ICCV)
- Agriculture technology conferences

### Science Competitions

**International Science and Engineering Fair (ISEF)**
- Prestigious high school competition
- Some undergrads eligible for special categories
- Significant prizes and recognition

**Regional Science Fairs**
- More accessible
- Can lead to ISEF qualification
- Good experience

### Symposia

**University Research Symposia**
- On-campus presentations
- Low-pressure environment
- Practice for external venues
- Recognition within institution

**State/Regional Symposia**
- State capitol research days
- Regional STEM conferences
- Professional society meetings

## Types of Publications

### Full Papers (6-8 pages)
**Content**: Complete research with literature review, methodology, results, analysis

**When to pursue**: Substantial, novel contribution with comprehensive evaluation

**Timeline**: 6-12 months from submission to publication

### Short Papers (2-4 pages)
**Content**: Work in progress, preliminary results, novel ideas

**When to pursue**: Interesting results but incomplete study, or innovative idea worth sharing early

**Timeline**: 3-6 months

### Extended Abstracts (1-2 pages)
**Content**: Summary of work, key results

**When to pursue**: Recent work you want to present quickly, or work already published elsewhere

**Timeline**: 1-3 months

### Posters
**Content**: Visual presentation of research

**When to pursue**: Any complete project, excellent for first-time presenters

**Timeline**: Submit abstract 1-2 months before conference

### Technical Reports
**Content**: Detailed documentation of system or project

**When to pursue**: Engineering projects, large systems, documentation focus

**Timeline**: Flexible, can self-publish

## The Publishing Process

### Step 1: Prepare Manuscript (4-8 weeks)

**Structure**:
1. **Abstract** (150-250 words)
   - Problem
   - Approach
   - Key results
   - Significance

2. **Introduction** (1-1.5 pages)
   - Motivation and problem
   - Research question
   - Contributions
   - Paper organization

3. **Related Work** (0.5-1 page)
   - Prior research
   - How your work differs
   - Position in field

4. **Methodology** (1.5-2 pages)
   - System description
   - Algorithm details
   - Implementation
   - Enough detail to reproduce

5. **Experimental Evaluation** (1.5-2 pages)
   - Experimental setup
   - Metrics
   - Results with figures/tables
   - Analysis and discussion

6. **Conclusion** (0.5 page)
   - Summary
   - Limitations
   - Future work

7. **References**
   - Comprehensive bibliography
   - Proper citations

**Writing Tips**:
- Clear, concise technical writing
- Active voice when possible
- Define acronyms on first use
- Consistent terminology
- Proofread multiple times
- Have others review

### Step 2: Select Venue (1 week)

**Consider**:
- **Prestige vs accessibility**: Balance ambition with realism
- **Timeline**: When do you need publication?
- **Audience**: Who should read this?
- **Cost**: Registration fees, travel
- **Format**: Paper vs poster

**Research the venue**:
- Read previous years' accepted papers
- Check acceptance rates
- Review formatting requirements
- Note deadlines

### Step 3: Format and Submit (1-2 weeks)

**Follow requirements exactly**:
- Page limits
- Template (LaTeX or Word)
- Figure formats
- Reference style
- Blind vs non-blind review

**Submission checklist**:
- ☐ Follows template precisely
- ☐ Within page limits
- ☐ Figures are clear and legible
- ☐ References properly formatted
- ☐ Proofread for typos
- ☐ Supplementary materials included
- ☐ Submission form completed

**Submit before deadline**:
- Don't wait until last minute
- Technical issues happen
- Time zones can be confusing

### Step 4: Review Period (2-4 months)

**What happens**:
- Paper assigned to reviewers (2-4 typically)
- Reviewers evaluate and provide feedback
- Editor/program committee makes decision

**Possible outcomes**:
- **Accept**: Congratulations!
- **Conditional accept**: Minor revisions needed
- **Revise and resubmit**: Major revisions, re-review
- **Reject**: Not accepted this time

**During review**:
- Work on other projects
- Prepare presentation (if confident)
- Don't contact editors asking for updates

### Step 5: Response to Reviews (1-2 weeks if revisions)

**If revisions requested**:
1. **Read reviews carefully**: Understand all comments
2. **Don't take personally**: Reviewers want to help
3. **Address every point**: Even if you disagree
4. **Create rebuttal document**: Point-by-point response
5. **Make changes**: Revise manuscript
6. **Resubmit promptly**: Respect deadline

**Handling criticism**:
- Reviewers are (usually) trying to improve your work
- Legitimate criticism helps strengthen paper
- If confused, ask mentor for interpretation
- Learn from feedback for next paper

### Step 6: Presentation Preparation (4-6 weeks)

**For oral presentation**:
- Create clear slides (not too text-heavy)
- Practice timing (usually 10-15 minutes + questions)
- Anticipate questions
- Rehearse multiple times

**For poster**:
- Design visually appealing poster (see below)
- Print high quality (check venue specifications)
- Prepare 2-minute elevator pitch
- Practice answering questions

**General tips**:
- Know your content deeply
- Speak clearly and slowly
- Make eye contact
- Show enthusiasm
- Welcome questions

### Step 7: Conference Attendance

**Before conference**:
- Register early (often cheaper)
- Book travel and accommodation
- Plan which talks to attend
- Prepare questions for other presenters
- Bring business cards (or equivalent)

**During conference**:
- Attend your session (obviously!)
- Network with others
- Ask questions at talks
- Visit poster sessions
- Attend social events
- Take notes on interesting work

**After conference**:
- Follow up with contacts
- Share your experience with research group
- Update CV with publication
- Write blog post or summary (optional)

## Creating Effective Posters

### Design Principles

**Layout**:
- Logical flow (top-left to bottom-right)
- Clear sections with headers
- White space (don't cram too much)
- Readable from 3-4 feet away

**Typography**:
- Title: 72-96pt
- Section headers: 36-48pt
- Body text: 24-32pt
- Sans-serif fonts (cleaner at distance)

**Color**:
- Consistent color scheme
- High contrast (dark text on light background usually best)
- Color blindness friendly
- Highlight key results with color

**Graphics**:
- Figures larger than in paper
- High resolution (300 dpi minimum)
- Annotate clearly
- More graphics, less text

### Content Structure

**Title Section**:
- Project title
- Authors and affiliations
- Institution logo
- Contact information

**Introduction** (brief):
- Problem/motivation
- Research question
- Key figure or diagram

**Methods** (concise):
- System overview diagram
- Key algorithms
- Just enough to understand

**Results** (emphasis here):
- Large, clear figures
- Key data visualizations
- Highlight main findings

**Conclusions**:
- Main takeaways (bullet points)
- Future work
- QR code to paper/project site

### Poster Session Tips

**Preparation**:
- Print extra copies of 1-page summary
- Bring business cards
- Prepare 2-minute "elevator pitch"
- Know your poster deeply

**During session**:
- Stand next to poster (be available)
- Make eye contact with passersby
- Offer brief summary, then let them read
- Ask if they have questions
- Exchange contact info if interested

**Common questions to prepare for**:
- "Tell me about your research"
- "What were your main findings?"
- "What surprised you?"
- "What's next for this project?"
- Technical questions about methodology

## Writing for Publication

### Technical Writing Best Practices

**Clarity**:
- Short sentences
- One idea per sentence
- Define terms before using
- Avoid jargon when possible

**Structure**:
- Logical flow between paragraphs
- Topic sentences preview paragraph
- Transitions between sections
- Parallel structure in lists

**Figures and Tables**:
- Every figure/table referenced in text
- Captions explain without text reference
- Labels and axis titles clear
- High quality (vector graphics when possible)

**Common mistakes**:
- Overuse of passive voice
- Undefined acronyms
- Figures without captions
- Missing citations
- Inconsistent terminology

### Citations and References

**When to cite**:
- Prior work you build on
- Methods you use
- Claims of fact
- Comparisons to other work

**How many**:
- Undergraduate papers: 10-20 references typical
- More is not always better
- Cite seminal works and recent advances

**Formats**:
- IEEE style common in robotics
- Use reference manager (Zotero, Mendeley)
- Be consistent

### LaTeX Tips

**Why LaTeX**:
- Professional appearance
- Easy equation formatting
- Automatic references/citations
- Required by most venues

**Learning LaTeX**:
- Overleaf (online, collaborative)
- Templates provided by conferences
- Takes a few hours to learn basics
- Worth the investment

**Useful packages**:
- graphicx (figures)
- amsmath (equations)
- algorithm2e (algorithms)
- hyperref (clickable links)

## Intellectual Property Considerations

### Before Publishing

**Check with advisor**:
- Any patentable inventions?
- Sponsor restrictions on publication?
- Need approval from institution?

**Open source code**:
- Choose appropriate license
- Clean up before releasing
- Include documentation

### After Publishing

**Copyright**:
- Understand what you're signing
- Many conferences allow arXiv preprints
- Keep copy for your records
- Can usually share on personal website

## Common Challenges

### "Our work isn't good enough"
**Reality**: Undergraduate work is evaluated differently. Solid execution of known methods with thorough evaluation is publishable.

### "We don't have time"
**Solution**: Start early, make it part of research timeline, get help with writing

### "We don't know how to write papers"
**Solution**: Read papers in venue you're targeting, work with mentor, multiple drafts

### "We got rejected"
**Reality**: Rejection is normal (even for experts). Learn from reviews and resubmit elsewhere.

### "We can't afford travel"
**Solution**: Apply for travel grants, choose regional conferences, virtual presentations
