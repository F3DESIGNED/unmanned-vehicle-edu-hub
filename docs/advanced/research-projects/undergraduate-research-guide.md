# Undergraduate Research in Unmanned Systems

A guide for students interested in conducting original research in unmanned vehicle systems.

## What is Undergraduate Research?

Undergraduate research involves investigating original questions, conducting experiments, analyzing data, and contributing new knowledge to the field. It's excellent preparation for graduate school and research careers.

## Research Process

### Phase 1: Topic Selection (2-4 weeks)
1. **Explore interests**: What problems fascinate you?
2. **Literature review**: What has been done?
3. **Identify gap**: What's missing or could be improved?
4. **Define question**: Specific, answerable research question
5. **Get approval**: Discuss with mentor/advisor

### Phase 2: Proposal Development (2-3 weeks)
1. **Background research**: Thorough literature review
2. **Methodology design**: How will you investigate?
3. **Timeline creation**: Realistic schedule
4. **Resource identification**: What do you need?
5. **Write proposal**: Formal research proposal

### Phase 3: Execution (8-16 weeks)
1. **Setup**: Prepare equipment, software, environment
2. **Initial experiments**: Proof of concept
3. **Data collection**: Systematic experimentation
4. **Iterative refinement**: Adjust based on results
5. **Documentation**: Detailed lab notebooks

### Phase 4: Analysis (2-4 weeks)
1. **Data processing**: Clean and organize data
2. **Statistical analysis**: Quantitative evaluation
3. **Result interpretation**: What do findings mean?
4. **Comparison**: How do results compare to prior work?
5. **Conclusions**: Answer research questions

### Phase 5: Communication (2-4 weeks)
1. **Paper writing**: Formal research paper
2. **Poster creation**: Visual presentation
3. **Talk preparation**: Oral presentation
4. **Submission**: Conference or journal submission
5. **Revision**: Respond to reviews

## Writing a Research Proposal

### Key Components

**1. Title**: Clear, descriptive, engaging

**2. Abstract** (200-300 words):
- Problem statement
- Proposed approach
- Expected impact

**3. Introduction**:
- Background and motivation
- Literature review
- Research gap
- Research questions/hypotheses
- Significance

**4. Methodology**:
- Experimental design
- Equipment and materials
- Procedures and protocols
- Data collection methods
- Analysis approaches

**5. Timeline**:
- Milestones and deliverables
- Realistic schedule
- Contingency plans

**6. Budget** (if applicable):
- Equipment costs
- Materials and supplies
- Travel (conferences)
- Justification

**7. Expected Outcomes**:
- Anticipated results
- Publications planned
- Broader impacts

**8. References**:
- Comprehensive bibliography
- Proper citation format

## Finding a Research Mentor

### Qualities of Good Mentors
- Active researcher in relevant area
- Available and responsive
- Supportive teaching style
- Good track record with students
- Resources and lab space

### Approaching Faculty
1. **Research their work**: Read recent papers
2. **Prepare questions**: Show genuine interest
3. **Professional email**: Clear, concise request
4. **Be flexible**: Adapt to their research directions
5. **Follow up**: Thank them regardless of outcome

```

Dear Professor [Name],

particularly fascinating. I would be interested in discussing potential
undergraduate research opportunities in your lab.

I am available [times] and would appreciate the opportunity to meet and
discuss your research program.

Thank you for your consideration.

[Your name]
[Contact information]
```

## Conducting Literature Reviews

### Finding Papers
- **Google Scholar**: Broad search
- **IEEE Xplore**: Engineering and robotics
- **arXiv**: Pre-prints and cutting-edge work
- **ResearchGate**: Contact authors directly
- **University library**: Access to paid journals

### Reading Strategies
1. **Abstract first**: Does it address your question?
2. **Skim structure**: Understand overall approach
3. **Read critically**: What are strengths/weaknesses?
4. **Take notes**: Summarize key points
5. **Track citations**: Find related work

### Organizing Literature
- Reference manager (Zotero, Mendeley)
- Annotated bibliography
- Concept maps linking papers
- Chronological timeline of developments

## Experimental Best Practices

### Documentation
- Detailed lab notebooks (date every entry)
- Version control for code
- Organized file structure
- Regular backups
- Clear naming conventions

### Reproducibility
- Document all parameters
- Save raw data
- Record environmental conditions
- Note equipment settings
- Track software versions

### Safety
- Risk assessment before experiments
- Safety equipment and procedures
- Proper training on equipment
- Supervision when required
- Emergency protocols

## Presenting Research

### Poster Presentations
- Clear visual hierarchy
- Minimal text, maximum graphics
- Logical flow (top-left to bottom-right)
- Readable from 3-4 feet away
- Practice elevator pitch (2 minutes)

### Oral Presentations
- Structure: Intro → Methods → Results → Conclusions
- Visual aids support, don't replace speech
- Practice timing (stay within limits)
- Anticipate questions
- Professional delivery

### Paper Writing
- Follow journal/conference format exactly
- Clear, concise technical writing
- Strong abstract (people read this first)
- Comprehensive but focused lit review
- Honest discussion of limitations
- Proofread multiple times

## Common Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge: Scope too broad
**Solution**: Narrow focus to specific, testable question

### Challenge: Equipment limitations
**Solution**: Simulation, collaboration, creative workarounds

### Challenge: Results don't match hypothesis
**Solution**: Negative results are still results! Analyze why

### Challenge: Time management
**Solution**: Set milestones, regular check-ins with mentor

### Challenge: Technical obstacles
**Solution**: Break problems down, seek help, try alternatives
