# Research Collaboration Framework

## UVS Education Hub - Pilot Program Research Partnerships

**Purpose:** This framework outlines opportunities for research collaboration, expectations for partners, and processes for engaging in research related to the UVS Education Hub pilot program.

---

## Overview

The UVS Education Hub welcomes research partnerships that:
- Advance knowledge about unmanned vehicle systems education
- Evaluate program effectiveness and impact
- Improve curriculum and instructional practices
- Contribute to broader understanding of STEM education
- Benefit participating students, teachers, and schools

**We seek mutually beneficial partnerships that balance research rigor with practical program needs.**

---

## Types of Research Partnerships

### Level 1: Program Evaluation Research (Built-In)
**Description:** Core evaluation research conducted by or on behalf of the UVS Education Hub

**Activities:**
- Implementation fidelity studies
- Student learning outcomes assessment
- Teacher professional development evaluation
- Program improvement research

**Partners:** Evaluation consultants, contracted researchers, university partners

**Access:** Full access to program data, schools, and participants

**Requirements:** Alignment with program evaluation goals, IRB approval, data sharing agreement

---

### Level 2: Collaborative Research Projects
**Description:** Joint research projects co-designed by UVS Education Hub and external researchers

**Examples:**
- Longitudinal studies of student outcomes
- Comparative effectiveness studies
- Mixed-methods case studies
- Curriculum development research

**Partners:** University faculty, research organizations, doctoral students

**Access:** Negotiated access to schools and data based on research design

**Requirements:** Collaborative planning, IRB approval, mutual benefit, shared authorship

---

### Level 3: Independent Research Projects
**Description:** Independent research by external researchers using pilot program as context

**Examples:**
- Dissertation studies
- Master's theses
- Faculty research projects
- Small-scale qualitative studies

**Partners:** Graduate students, university faculty, independent researchers

**Access:** Limited access based on program capacity and school willingness

**Requirements:** Proposal review and approval, IRB, minimal burden on participants, data sharing

---

### Level 4: Secondary Data Analysis
**Description:** Analysis of existing program data for research purposes

**Examples:**
- Meta-analyses
- Literature reviews
- Secondary analyses of assessment data
- Program improvement studies

**Partners:** Any qualified researchers

**Access:** De-identified data sets after cohort completion

**Requirements:** Data use agreement, IRB (if applicable), credit to UVS program

---

## Research Priorities and Topics

### High-Priority Research Questions

**Student Learning and Development:**
- What do students learn through the UVS curriculum?
- How does participation affect STEM interest and self-efficacy?
- What factors predict student success?
- How do impacts vary by student demographics?

**Implementation and Fidelity:**
- What does high-quality implementation look like?
- What factors support or hinder implementation?
- How do adaptations affect outcomes?
- What resources and supports are essential?

**Teacher Professional Development:**
- How do teachers develop content knowledge and pedagogical skills?
- What PD structures are most effective?
- How does teaching this curriculum affect teacher practice?

**Equity and Access:**
- How can the program effectively serve underrepresented students?
- What barriers to participation exist and how can they be addressed?
- How does impact vary across diverse contexts?

**Scalability and Sustainability:**
- What conditions enable program sustainability?
- How can the program scale while maintaining quality?
- What is the cost-effectiveness?

### Additional Research Topics of Interest

- Career pathway development and college/career outcomes
- Partnership models (industry, higher education, community)
- Integration with other STEM programs
- Technology and equipment considerations
- Assessment and evaluation methods
- Policy implications and recommendations

## Partnership Process

### Step 1: Initial Inquiry

**Researchers interested in collaboration should:**
- Review this framework and research priorities
- Submit initial inquiry form (see below)
- Include brief research proposal (1-2 pages)

**Submit to:** research@uvs-edu-hub.org

**Response timeline:** Within 2 weeks

---

### Step 2: Proposal Development

**If initial inquiry is promising:**
- Schedule consultation call with program team
- Develop full research proposal using template provided
- Address practical and ethical considerations
- Identify needed resources and support

**Proposal should address:**
- Research questions and significance
- Theoretical framework and literature
- Methods and design
- Data collection needs (who, what, when, how)
- Timeline
- IRB approval plan
- Burden on participants and mitigation strategies
- Dissemination plans
- Mutual benefits

---

### Step 3: Review and Approval

**Proposals reviewed by:**
- UVS Education Hub program director
- Evaluation team lead
- External advisory board (for major studies)
- Participating schools (as appropriate)

**Review criteria:**
- Alignment with program priorities
- Research quality and rigor
- Feasibility and appropriateness
- Burden on participants
- Ethical considerations
- Mutual benefit
- Resources required

**Timeline:** 4-6 weeks for review and decision

**Decisions:** Approve, approve with modifications, defer, decline

---

### Step 4: Agreements and Approvals

**If approved, partners must:**
- Execute data sharing agreement (template provided)
- Obtain IRB approval from home institution
- Obtain district/school IRB approval if required
- Execute MOU or contract if applicable
- Coordinate consent/assent processes

---

### Step 5: Data Collection and Implementation

**During research:**
- Coordination through designated liaison
- Regular communication and updates
- Adherence to approved protocols
- Responsive to participant concerns
- Minimal disruption to program activities

---

### Step 6: Analysis and Dissemination

**Researchers commit to:**
- Share preliminary findings with program team
- Opportunity for program team to provide feedback
- Co-authorship for collaborative projects
- Credit and acknowledgment of program
- Provide copies of publications to program
- Present findings to pilot school community (optional)
- Share de-identified data (when appropriate)

---

## Requirements for Research Partners

### All Partners Must:

**1. Ethical Conduct:**
- Obtain and maintain current IRB approval
- Follow all ethical guidelines for research with human subjects
- Protect participant privacy and confidentiality
- Obtain informed consent/assent appropriately
- Minimize burden and risk to participants

**2. FERPA Compliance:**
- Understand and comply with FERPA regulations
- Use proper data security measures
- Limit data access to authorized personnel
- Report any data breaches immediately

**3. Cultural Sensitivity:**
- Respect school cultures and contexts
- Be mindful of participant time and workload
- Communicate clearly and respectfully
- Be responsive to participant concerns

**4. Data Sharing and Security:**
- Execute data sharing agreement
- Use secure data transmission and storage
- Limit data retention to what is necessary
- Return or destroy data per agreement

**5. Communication:**
- Maintain regular contact with program liaison
- Provide updates on progress
- Communicate any issues or changes promptly
- Coordinate visit schedules in advance

**6. Dissemination:**
- Share findings with program team
- Acknowledge UVS Education Hub appropriately
- Provide copies of all publications
- Respect embargo periods if applicable

## Support for Research Partners

**The UVS Education Hub can provide:**

**Access:**
- Introduction to pilot schools
- Coordination of data collection activities
- Existing program data (with agreement)

**Resources:**
- Background on program and curriculum
- Existing assessments and instruments
- Recruitment support (within limits)
- Space in program communications for recruitment

**Collaboration:**
- Consultation on research design
- Feedback on instruments
- Collaboration on interpretation
- Co-authorship opportunities (for collaborative projects)

**What we cannot provide:**
- Funding for research (generally)
- Guarantee of school participation
- Unlimited access or demands on participants
- Proprietary curriculum materials for distribution

---

## School Participation in Research

### School Rights and Considerations

**Schools may:**
- Decline to participate in any research project
- Set parameters for researcher access
- Request modifications to protocols
- Withdraw from research at any time
- Review findings before publication (for case studies)

**Schools should consider:**
- Burden on teachers and students
- Alignment with school priorities
- Potential benefits of participation
- Data privacy and security
- Timing and logistics

**Incentives for participation:**
- Early access to findings
- Potential publication/recognition
- Contribution to field knowledge
- Networking with researchers
- Possible small stipends (project-dependent)

---

## Data Access and Sharing

### Program Data Available for Research

**With appropriate agreements, researchers may access:**
- Student assessment data (de-identified)
- Teacher survey and reflection data (anonymized)
- Implementation data (attendance, completion rates, etc.)
- Demographic data (aggregated or de-identified)
- Observation notes (with permission)
- Student work samples (with releases)

**Restrictions:**
- No personally identifiable information shared without explicit consent
- Data shared only for approved research purposes
- Security requirements must be met
- Data use must comply with FERPA

**Process:**
- Submit data request specifying needs
- Execute data sharing agreement
- Provide IRB approval documentation
- Receive data in de-identified format
- Acknowledge source in publications

---

## Intellectual Property and Authorship

### Collaborative Projects
- Co-authorship for jointly designed and conducted research
- Author order determined by contribution
- All authors review and approve manuscripts
- Credit to UVS Education Hub in acknowledgments

### Independent Projects
- Researcher is primary author
- Acknowledgment of UVS program and support
- Opportunity for program team to review and provide feedback
- Optional inclusion of program staff as authors if substantive contribution

### Use of Curriculum Materials
- Curriculum materials are proprietary to UVS Education Hub
- Permission required for reproduction or distribution
- Citation required for references to curriculum
- Derivative works require explicit permission

---

## Research Timeline and Cycles

### Best Times for Research Activities

**Fall (September-November):**
- Program planning and teacher preparation
- Baseline data collection
- Recruitment activities

**Spring (January-May):**
- Active implementation
- Classroom observations
- Student data collection
- Teacher interviews (avoid first few weeks)

**Summer (June-August):**
- Teacher interviews and focus groups
- Data analysis
- In-depth case studies
- Professional development research

**Avoid:**
- First 2 weeks of program implementation
- Final 2 weeks (end-of-course assessments and projects)
- School testing periods
- Major holidays

## Examples of Successful Research Partnerships

### Example 1: University Partnership - Student Outcomes Study
**Partner:** State University College of Education
**Type:** Collaborative research
**Focus:** Longitudinal study of student learning and career interest
**Activities:** Pre/post assessments, student interviews, tracking alumni
**Benefit:** Rigorous evidence of impact, joint publications, student research experience

### Example 2: Dissertation Research - Implementation Fidelity
**Partner:** Doctoral student in STEM Education
**Type:** Independent research
**Focus:** Factors affecting curriculum implementation fidelity
**Activities:** Classroom observations, teacher interviews, document analysis
**Benefit:** In-depth understanding of implementation, fresh perspective, publication

### Example 3: Evaluation Partnership - Program Improvement
**Partner:** Educational research organization
**Type:** Program evaluation
**Focus:** Comprehensive program evaluation for continuous improvement
**Activities:** Multi-method evaluation across all pilot schools
**Benefit:** Rigorous external evaluation, recommendations for improvement, credibility
