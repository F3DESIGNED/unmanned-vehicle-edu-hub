# IRB Guidance for Pilot Program Research

## Overview

Research involving pilot program participants requires Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval to ensure ethical conduct and protection of human subjects. This document provides guidance for researchers and schools navigating the IRB process.

---

## Who Needs IRB Approval?

**Researchers from universities/institutions:**
- Must obtain IRB approval from home institution
- Follow institutional IRB procedures and timelines

**School district research:**
- May require district IRB or research review committee approval
- Check with district research coordinator

**UVS Program team:**
- Program evaluation activities conducted under program's IRB
- External collaborators added to program's IRB when appropriate

---

## Types of Review

### Exempt Research
**May qualify if:**
- Uses existing de-identified data
- Educational research in normal educational settings
- Survey/interview research on non-sensitive topics

**Process:** Usually expedited, less paperwork

### Expedited Review
**May qualify if:**
- Minimal risk to participants
- Standard educational research methods
- Common assessment and survey procedures

**Process:** Reviewed by IRB chair or designee, faster than full board

### Full Board Review
**Required if:**
- More than minimal risk
- Vulnerable populations (beyond normal education context)
- Innovative or non-standard procedures

**Process:** Monthly board meetings, longer timeline

**Most pilot program research will qualify for exempt or expedited review.**

## IRB Application Components

### 1. Research Proposal

**Include:**
- Research questions and objectives
- Significance and background
- Methods and procedures
- Participant selection and recruitment
- Data collection instruments
- Data analysis plan
- Timeline

**For pilot program research:**
- Describe partnership with UVS Education Hub
- Explain integration with program activities
- Clarify role of program team vs. researcher

---

### 2. Risk-Benefit Analysis

**Minimal risks typically:**
- Time and inconvenience
- Minor discomfort answering questions
- Breach of confidentiality (mitigated by security measures)
- Social risks (minimal in educational research)

**Benefits typically:**
- Contribution to knowledge
- Program improvement
- Potential indirect benefit to participants
- Educational experience for students/teachers

**For pilot program:**
- Risks generally minimal for well-designed studies
- Benefits include improving educational programs

---

### 3. Informed Consent Documents

**Parent/Guardian Consent (for students under 18):**
- Simple language (8th grade reading level)
- Purpose of research
- Procedures and time required
- Risks and benefits
- Voluntary nature
- Confidentiality protections
- Right to withdraw
- Contact information
- FERPA statement

**Student Assent (for students able to provide):**
- Age-appropriate language
- Brief explanation
- Voluntary participation
- Right to stop anytime

**Teacher/Staff Consent:**
- Similar to parent consent
- Professional considerations

**See consent form templates in data-collection-protocols folder.**

---

### 4. Recruitment Materials

**Submit all recruitment materials to IRB:**
- Flyers
- Emails
- Scripts for announcements
- Letters to parents

**Guidelines:**
- Not coercive
- Accurate description
- Clear voluntary nature
- Appropriate to audience

---

### 5. Data Collection Instruments

**Submit copies of:**
- Surveys and questionnaires
- Interview guides and protocols
- Observation protocols
- Assessment tools

**IRB reviews for:**
- Appropriateness for population
- Sensitive or invasive questions
- Clarity and understandability
- Burden on participants

---

### 6. Data Security Plan

**Address:**
- How data will be collected (paper, electronic, etc.)
- Where data will be stored
- Who will have access
- Security measures (encryption, passwords, locked files)
- Data retention and destruction
- De-identification procedures

**For pilot program data:**
- Coordination with program data security
- Use of program's secure systems when possible
- Additional protections for sensitive data

---

### 7. FERPA Compliance

**For educational records:**
- Explain FERPA compliance
- De-identification procedures
- Limitations on data sharing
- School district policies

**IRB will want to know:**
- Is data from educational records?
- How will FERPA be addressed?
- What permissions/waivers are needed?

---

## Special Considerations for Pilot Program Research

### 1. Program vs. Research Activities

**Distinguish between:**
- Normal program evaluation (part of pilot participation)
- Additional research activities (require separate consent)

**Example:**
- Pre/post assessments = program evaluation (covered by program consent)
- Additional student interviews = research (need separate consent)

**Strategy:**
- Clearly differentiate in consent forms
- Allow opt-out of research while continuing program participation

---

### 2. Multiple IRBs

**May need approval from:**
- Researcher's institution
- School district
- Partner institution (if applicable)
- UVS program IRB (if accessing program data)

**Strategies:**
- IRB Authorization Agreement (rely on single IRB)
- Reciprocity agreements between institutions
- Coordinate applications to reduce duplication

---

### 3. Vulnerable Populations

**Students are considered vulnerable because:**
- Minors (under 18)
- Educational setting (power dynamics)

**Additional protections:**
- Parent consent required (under 18)
- Student assent obtained
- No penalty for non-participation
- Teacher/researcher not involved in grading (when possible)
- Private setting for data collection

---

### 4. Teacher Participants

**Teachers are NOT vulnerable populations generally**

**But consider:**
- Employment relationship if research by administrator
- Voluntary participation without coercion
- Confidentiality from supervisors

---

## Timeline for IRB Approval

**Plan for these timelines:**

**Exempt review:** 2-4 weeks
**Expedited review:** 4-6 weeks
**Full board review:** 6-8 weeks (depends on meeting schedule)

**Add time for:**
- Preparing application: 2-4 weeks
- Institutional review before submission: 1-2 weeks
- Revisions if requested: 2-4 weeks
- District approval (if required): 2-8 weeks

**Total: Plan for 3-6 months from start to approval**

**Coordinate with pilot program timeline - start early!**

## Common IRB Questions and Issues

**Q: Do I need consent from students if data is de-identified?**
A: If data is truly de-identified and poses no risk, may be exempt. But if you're collecting data directly from students, usually need consent/assent.

**Q: Can I use data collected by the program?**
A: Yes, with data sharing agreement and IRB approval for secondary data use.

**Q: What if school district doesn't have an IRB?**
A: Researcher's IRB can serve, but district research coordinator should approve access.

**Q: Can parents opt out of research but student stay in program?**
A: Yes! Always offer this option.

**Q: Do I need consent for classroom observations?**
A: Depends. If observing teaching (not focusing on individual students), may not need student consent. Check with IRB.

## Modifications and Continuing Review

### Protocol Modifications

**Need IRB approval for:**
- Changes to procedures
- New data collection instruments
- Changes to consent forms
- Adding research sites or participants

**Process:**
- Submit amendment to IRB
- Wait for approval before implementing changes

### Continuing Review

**Annual review required for:**
- Studies longer than one year
- Some expedited protocols

**Process:**
- Submit progress report
- Update on enrollment, data collection, issues
- Confirm continued appropriateness of procedures
