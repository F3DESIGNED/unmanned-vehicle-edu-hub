# STEM Interest Survey

## Administration Instructions

**Purpose:** Measure student interest in STEM fields and how it changes through participation in the UVS program.

**When to administer:**
- **Pre-survey:** First week of course (before instruction)
- **Post-survey:** Last week of course (after instruction)

**Format:** Likert scale survey (1-5)
**Time:** 10-15 minutes
**Confidential:** No names, use student ID codes only

**Instructions to students:**
"This survey asks about your interests in science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM). There are no right or wrong answers. Be honest about how you feel. Your responses are confidential and will not affect your grade."

## Scoring Guide

### Subscales and Items

**General STEM Interest (Items 1-10):**
- Sum of items 1-10
- Range: 10-50
- Higher scores = greater STEM interest

**Unmanned Systems Interest (Items 11-15):**
- Sum of items 11-15
- Range: 5-25
- Higher scores = greater interest in UVS

**STEM Self-Efficacy (Items 16-23):**
- Sum of items 16-23
- Range: 8-40
- Higher scores = greater confidence in STEM abilities

**STEM Career Interest (Items 24-30):**
- Sum of items 24-30
- Range: 7-35
- Higher scores = greater interest in STEM careers

**Total STEM Interest Score:**
- Sum of all items 1-30
- Range: 30-150
- Higher scores = more positive STEM attitudes overall

## Interpretation and Use

### Pre-Survey

**Use to:**
- Establish baseline STEM attitudes
- Identify students with high/low initial interest
- Inform instructional approach (e.g., emphasize connections for low-interest students)

**Typical pre-survey patterns:**
- Wide variation in interest levels
- Some students may have low self-efficacy despite interest
- Career interest often lower than general interest

### Post-Survey

**Use to:**
- Measure change in attitudes
- Calculate pre/post gains for individuals and groups
- Evaluate program impact on attitudes
- Identify persistent barriers to STEM interest

**Expected changes:**
- Increase in Unmanned Systems Interest (program-specific)
- Increase in STEM Self-Efficacy (confidence from hands-on success)
- Moderate increases in General STEM Interest and Career Interest
- Some students may show decrease (normal variation, overly high pre-survey)

### Analyzing Change

**Calculate:**
- Individual gain scores (post minus pre for each subscale)
- Group mean pre, post, and gain
- % students showing increase, no change, decrease
- Effect size (Cohen's d)

**Meaningful change:**
- Individual: 5+ point increase on subscale
- Group: Mean gain of 3+ points, effect size d > 0.3

---

## Validity and Reliability

**Content validity:**
- Items adapted from validated STEM attitude instruments
- Aligned with program goals and theory of change

**Construct validity:**
- Four distinct but related constructs (interest, UVS-specific, efficacy, career)
- Expected correlations among subscales

**Reliability:**
- Target Cronbach's α > 0.80 for each subscale
- Pilot testing to refine items
- Test-retest reliability assessed

**Sensitivity:**
- Designed to detect changes over one semester
- Avoid ceiling effects (items range across difficulty)
