# Cost Reduction Strategies

Practical ways to reduce costs and stretch program budgets without sacrificing quality.

## Equipment Costs

### Buy Smart
- **Refurbished drones**: 30-50% savings, manufacturer certified
- **Educational discounts**: Many manufacturers offer 10-20% off
- **Bulk purchases**: Negotiate discounts for multiple units
- **Previous generation**: Often 40-60% less, still capable
- **Package deals**: Bundles with batteries, props, cases

### Extend Lifespan
- Proper maintenance schedules
- Protective cases and storage
- Spare parts inventory
- Student maintenance crew
- Repair skills training

### Creative Sourcing
- Equipment donations from industry
- Parent/community donations
- Used equipment from other programs
- Grant-funded purchases
- Lease-to-own options

## Operating Costs

### Reduce Consumables
- Prop guards reduce damage
- Proper training reduces crashes
- Simulator practice before real flights
- Quality batteries last longer
- Buy consumables in bulk

### Share Resources
- District-wide equipment pool
- Regional consortium
- Borrow specialized equipment
- Share facility costs
- Collaborative purchasing

### Free/Low-Cost Alternatives
- Open-source software vs. paid
- Free simulation environments
- DIY obstacle courses vs. purchased
- Community indoor spaces
- Virtual competitions

## Personnel Costs

### Leverage Existing Staff
- Professional development for current teachers
- Student teaching assistants
- Parent/community volunteers
- Industry mentors (volunteer)
- Remote experts (video calls)

### Efficient Staffing
- After-school clubs vs. full courses initially
- Combined classes or electives
- Student peer teaching
- Differentiated instruction (mixed levels)
- Online components for flexibility

## Facility Costs

### Use What You Have
- Existing classroom space
- Gymnasium for indoor flying
- Parking lot for outdoor practice
- Cafeteria for events
- Library for planning/design

### Partner for Space
- Local businesses (indoor space)
- Parks and recreation facilities
- Community centers
- Other schools (off-hours)
- Churches or civic organizations

## Curriculum & Materials

### Open Resources
- This free curriculum!
- MIT OpenCourseWare
- NASA educational resources
- FAA free materials
- Open-source projects

### Reduce Printing
- Digital materials vs. printed
- Reusable worksheets (laminated)
- Shared online documents
- Videos instead of handouts
- QR codes to online resources

## Insurance & Liability

### Risk Reduction
- Excellent safety record = lower premiums
- Bundle with existing school insurance
- Shop multiple providers
- Higher deductibles (if comfortable)
- Safety measures documentation

### Alternatives
- Manufacturer insurance options
- Professional association coverage
- Self-insurance for small programs
- Risk pooling with other programs

## Competition & Travel

### Reduce Travel Costs
- Virtual competitions
- Regional vs. national events
- Fundraising for travel
- Parent/business sponsorships
- Group rates and early booking

### Maximize Experience
- Compete in multiple categories
- Attend conferences when nearby
- Combine multiple events in one trip
- Parent chaperones (vs. paid staff)
- Virtual participation options

## Budget Scenarios with Reductions

### Micro Budget: Cut $2,000 → $1,000
- Start with 2 refurbished drones instead of 3 new ($800 savings)
- Use free curriculum resources ($200 savings)
- DIY obstacles and targets ($200 savings)
- Borrow extras from other programs ($300 savings)
- Delay insurance first semester ($500 savings)

### Small Budget: Cut $8,000 → $5,000
- Previous generation drones ($1,500 savings)
- Free software vs. paid licenses ($500 savings)
- Volunteer mentors vs. paid ($1,000 savings)
- Existing space vs. facility fees ($500 savings)
- Virtual competition year 1 ($500 savings)

## Hidden Cost Savings

**Energy efficient practices**:
- Charge overnight (off-peak)
- Turn off equipment when not in use
- Use power strips

**Preventive maintenance**:
- Regular inspections catch problems early
- Proper storage extends battery life
- Trained students reduce damage

**Documentation**:
- Track everything for tax deductions
- Document donations for thank-yous
- Photo/video evidence for grants

## False Economies (Don't Cut These)

### Safety
- Never compromise on safety equipment
- Insurance is essential
- Training must be thorough
- Supervision ratios matter

### Quality of Experience
- Adequate equipment for all students
- Some competitions/events are worth it
- Professional development for teachers
- Student recognition and celebration

## Case Studies

### Program A: Micro Budget Success
Started with $1,500, grew to $8,000 program
- Key strategies: refurbished equipment, volunteer mentors, creative partnerships
- Result: Served 25 students year 1, 60 students year 3

### Program B: Strategic Cuts
Faced 30% budget cut, maintained quality
- Key strategies: equipment sharing, eliminated travel, increased donations
- Result: Served same students with reduced costs

---

**Remember**: The goal is sustainable quality, not just being cheap. Strategic cost reduction lets you serve more students and grow the program.
