# Getting Started with Video Production

You don't need expensive equipment or professional skills to create valuable educational videos. Start simple and improve over time.

## Mindset

**Remember:**
- Content quality > production quality
- Your expertise is valuable
- Imperfect videos are better than no videos
- Viewers are forgiving of amateur production
- You'll improve with practice

## Start Here: Phone Videos

**Your smartphone is enough to start:**
- 1080p or better resolution
- Built-in stabilization (newer phones)
- Decent microphone
- Free editing apps available

**Quick start guide:**
1. Clean your lens
2. Shoot horizontal (landscape)
3. Find good lighting (face a window)
4. Use headphone mic if available
5. Speak clearly and pace yourself
6. Film multiple takes (editing insurance)
7. Keep videos under 10 minutes when starting

## Essential Equipment (Priority Order)

### Must Have: Audio
**Why:** Bad audio ruins good video. Good audio saves bad video.

**Start with:** $20-30 lavalier (lapel) mic for phone/camera
**Upgrade to:** $50-100 USB microphone for screencast

### Should Have: Lighting
**Why:** Good lighting makes you look professional, makes content clear

**Start with:** Film facing a window (free!)
**Upgrade to:** $30-50 LED panel or ring light

### Nice to Have: Tripod
**Why:** Stable, consistent framing

**Start with:** Prop phone against books
**Upgrade to:** $20-40 phone/camera tripod

### Eventually: Camera
**Why:** Better than phone only if you need specific features

**Only upgrade when:** Phone limitations become actual barriers

## Types of Videos You Can Make

### Screencast (Easiest!)
**Equipment:** Computer, mic, screencast software (free options exist)
**Best for:** Software tutorials, configuration demos, presentations
**Tools:** OBS Studio (free), Loom, QuickTime (Mac)

### Talking Head
**Equipment:** Phone/camera, mic, basic lighting
**Best for:** Explanations, introductions, concept teaching
**Setup time:** 15-30 minutes

### Hands-On Demonstration
**Equipment:** Phone/camera on tripod, good overhead/task lighting
**Best for:** Building, assembly, troubleshooting
**Setup time:** 30-60 minutes
**Tip:** Use overhead camera angle

### Mix of Screencast + Talking Head
**Equipment:** Both setups
**Best for:** Comprehensive tutorials
**Editing:** More complex but very effective

## Video Planning (Don't Skip This!)

**Before filming:**
1. Write script or detailed outline
2. Gather all materials
3. Test equipment
4. Practice once through
5. Note timing
6. Identify potential problems

**Planning saves time:** 30 min planning saves hours of filming/editing

## Filming Tips

**Setup:**
- Clean, uncluttered background
- Good lighting on your face
- Camera at eye level
- Frame with headroom (don't cut off top of head)
- Test audio levels first

**During filming:**
- Speak 20% slower than conversation
- Pause between sections (easier editing)
- If you mess up, pause, then restart from that point
- Film intro/outro separately
- Get B-roll (extra footage of demonstrations)

**Energy:**
- More energy than you think you need
- Smile (yes, even for technical videos)
- Vary your tone and pace
- Show genuine enthusiasm

## Basic Editing

**Free tools:**
- DaVinci Resolve (powerful, free)
- iMovie (Mac)
- OpenShot (cross-platform)
- Shotcut (cross-platform)

**Start with basics:**
- Cut mistakes and long pauses
- Add simple intro/outro
- Add text overlays for key points
- Normalize audio levels
- Export at 1080p

**Don't worry about:**
- Complex transitions (cuts are fine)
- Motion graphics (later skill)
- Color grading (not necessary)
- Perfection (good enough is good enough)

## Captions (REQUIRED)

**Why mandatory:**
- Accessibility for deaf/hard of hearing
- Helpful for non-native speakers
- Many watch with sound off
- Improves comprehension
- Community requirement

**How to add:**
- YouTube auto-captions (then edit for accuracy)
- Rev.com ($1.25/min, professional)
- Otter.ai (free tier available)
- Manual (time-consuming but free)

**See:** [Caption Requirements](accessibility/caption-requirements.md)

## Publishing

**Where to host:**
- YouTube (recommended - free, reliable, searchable)
- Vimeo (cleaner interface, limited free tier)
- Community may host (for official content)

**Video description must include:**
- Clear description of content
- Timestamps/chapters
- Links to resources
- Prerequisites
- Equipment list (if applicable)
- Community links

## Your First Video Challenge

**Make this video:**
- Topic: Introduce yourself and your program
- Length: 2-3 minutes
- Equipment: Phone, natural light
- Content: Who you are, what you teach, why you're excited
- Edit: Cut out mistakes, add text overlay with your name
- Share: Post in Show & Tell

**Goal:** Practice basics without pressure

## Common Beginner Mistakes

**Avoid:**
- Apologizing for production quality (just start)
- Over-editing first videos (done > perfect)
- Buying equipment before making first video
- Making videos too long (shorter is better)
- Not using captions (required!)
- Giving up after first attempt
